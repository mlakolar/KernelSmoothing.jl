Local polynomial regression

fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y) fits local polynomials at a set of points and estimates the regression function or one of its derivatives, with conventional and robust bias-corrected standard errors and confidence intervals (Calonico, Cattaneo and Farrell 2018). With given bandwidths, the results agree with nprobust to rounding.

using KernelSmoothing

x = rand(2000); y = sin.(2π .* x) .+ 0.3 .* randn(2000)
m = fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y; points = range(0, 1; length = 21))
coef(m)                                  # the estimates at the points
confint(m)                               # robust bias-corrected 95% intervals
confint(m; method = Conventional())      # intervals that ignore the bias
predict(m, [0.25, 0.5])                  # estimates at new points
coeftable(m)                             # everything, as a table of columns
fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y; derivative = 1)              # the derivative, by local quadratics

The estimator

Let $(x_i, y_i)$ be the observations with weights $w_i$, $K$ a kernel and $h$ a bandwidth. At a point $x_0$, the fit of order $p$ minimizes

\[\sum_{i=1}^n w_i K\Big(\frac{x_i - x_0}{h}\Big) \Big( y_i - \sum_{j=0}^p \beta_j (x_i - x_0)^j \Big)^2 ,\]

and $\nu!\, \hat\beta_\nu$ estimates the derivative $m^{(\nu)}(x_0)$ of the regression function for $0 \leq \nu \leq p$. In the scaled variable $u = (x - x_0)/h$, with $r_p(u) = (1, u, \dots, u^p)'$ and the Gram matrix $\Gamma_p = \sum_i w_i K(u_i) r_p(u_i) r_p(u_i)'$, the estimate is linear in the responses, $\sum_i \ell_i y_i$ with $\ell_i = (\nu!/h^\nu)\, w_i K(u_i)\, e_\nu' \Gamma_p^{-1} r_p(u_i)$.

The leading bias of the fit is $\Gamma_p^{-1} \Lambda\, \beta_{p+1}$, where $\Lambda = \sum_i w_i K(u_i) u_i^{p+1} r_p(u_i)$ and $\beta_{p+1}$ is the next Taylor coefficient (Calonico, Cattaneo and Titiunik 2014). We estimate $\beta_{p+1}$ by a fit of order $q = p + 1$ with the pilot bandwidth $b$, in the variable $v = (x - x_0)/b$. The bias-corrected estimate is then $\sum_i \ell_i^{\mathrm{bc}} y_i$ with

\[\ell_i^{\mathrm{bc}} = \ell_i - \frac{\nu!}{h^\nu} \Big(\frac{h}{b}\Big)^{p+1} (g' \Lambda)\, w_i K(v_i)\, c' r_q(v_i), \qquad g = \Gamma_p^{-1} e_\nu, \qquad c = \Gamma_q^{-1} e_{p+1} ,\]

where $\Gamma_q$ is the Gram matrix of the second fit. The standard errors are $(\sum_i \ell_i^2 \hat\sigma_i^2)^{1/2}$ and $(\sum_i (\ell_i^{\mathrm{bc}})^2 \hat\sigma_i^2)^{1/2}$, so that the robust one includes the variability of the bias estimate and its covariance with the estimate. By default $b = h$, as in nprobust, and then the bias-corrected estimate is the fit of order $p + 1$ at $h$.

The conditional variances $\hat\sigma_i^2$ come from NNVariance, the default, which compares each response with the mean of its nearest neighbours in the window, or from HCVariance, which uses the residuals of the local fits with the corrections HC0 to HC3. In small samples at the boundary, HC0 understates the variance more than the nearest-neighbour estimator and HC3.

For observations that are independent across clusters but may be dependent within them, pass the cluster of each observation as cluster. The default variance estimator is then ClusterVariance(1), the CR1 estimator of nprobust, which squares the sums of $\ell_i \hat\varepsilon_i$ over the clusters in the window instead of the single products, with the factor $(n - 1) / (n - k) \cdot G / (G - 1)$ for $n$ observations and $G$ clusters in the window and $k$ coefficients. Types 2 and 3 adjust the residuals of each cluster with its block of the hat matrix, as Bell and McCaffrey (2002) do for CR2, and type 0 leaves them as they are. The covariances of vcov and the bands of confband sum over clusters in the same way, and so do the variance constants of the plug-in bandwidths. With one observation per cluster, types 0 to 2 reduce to HCVariance of the same type.

At each point, the bandwidths are raised where needed to the distance of the minobs-th nearest observation, 21 by default as nprobust's bwcheck. With ties, such a window can still hold too few distinct values: the fit of order $p$ needs $p + 1$ distinct values of positive weight and the bias correction $p + 2$. Where they are missing, the bandwidth is raised further, to 1.5 times the distance of the farthest distinct value it needs, which puts that value well inside the window; with compat = true, as in nprobust, it is not. The fit records the points where these floors bind. Estimates that still lack distinct values, as with minobs = 0 or with too few distinct values in the data, are NaN, so that the conventional estimate survives a missing bias correction, and coeftable reports the number of distinct values at each point. The default degree is derivative + 1, as in nprobust, since an odd $p - \nu$ has the same order of bias at the boundary as in the interior.

The default bandwidths follow nprobust: the plug-in bandwidth MSEOptimal at a single point and IMSEOptimal at several, with pilot bandwidths anchored on cross-validation; see Bandwidth selection.

Local polynomial smoothing

For smoothing many curves, or for building other estimators on local fits, localpolynomial gives the estimates of the local polynomial fits at a set of points with a given bandwidth, without the floors on the windows and without standard errors, from kernelsums for all points at once. smoothermatrix gives their weights: each estimate is a weighted sum of the responses, L * y, with columns in the input order of the observations, so that linear functionals of the fit, hat matrices and their traces follow directly.

e = localpolynomial(t, y, grid; bandwidth = 0.1)                 # a smoothed curve on a grid
L = smoothermatrix(t, t; bandwidth = 0.1)                        # the hat matrix of the observations
gcv = length(y) * sum(abs2, y - L * y) / (length(y) - sum(LinearAlgebra.diag(L)))^2

For a regression function of two covariates, as the covariance surfaces of functional data, localpolynomial((x1, x2), y, (g1, g2); bandwidth) gives the bivariate local constant or local linear estimates on a grid, with a product kernel and a bandwidth for each covariate, and smoothermatrix((x1, x2), points) their weights. selectbandwidth(CrossValidation(), (x1, x2), y) chooses the bandwidth by leave-one-out cross-validation, or with groups, as the curves of functional data, by leaving out whole groups, so that the dependence within a curve does not lead to too small bandwidths.

s = selectbandwidth(CrossValidation(), (t1, t2), products; groups = curve)
C = localpolynomial((t1, t2), products, (grid, grid); bandwidth = s.h)   # a covariance surface
KernelSmoothing.ClusterVariance — Type
ClusterVariance(type = 1)

Cluster-robust variances, for observations that are independent across clusters but may be dependent within them. The variance of an estimate ∑ ℓᵢ yᵢ is estimated by c ∑_g (∑_{i ∈ g} ℓᵢ ε̃ᵢ)², a sum over the clusters g with observations in the window, where ε̃ᵢ are the residuals ε̂ᵢ of the local fit, adjusted for type = 2 and type = 3:

  • type = 0: ε̃ = ε̂ and c = 1;
  • type = 1 (CR1, the default): ε̃ = ε̂ and c = (n - 1) / (n - k) ⋅ G / (G - 1);
  • type = 2 (CR2, Bell and McCaffrey 2002): ε̃_g = (I - H_gg)^(-1/2) ε̂_g and c = 1;
  • type = 3 (CR3): ε̃_g = (I - H_gg)^(-1) ε̂_g and c = (G - 1) / G.

Here n is the number of observations and G the number of clusters in the window, k the number of coefficients, ε̂_g the residuals of cluster g, and H_gg its block of the hat matrix of the local fit. The clusters come with the data, fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y; cluster) or SmoothingData(x, y; cluster), and with clusters the default variance estimator is ClusterVariance(1), as in nprobust. As for HCVariance, conventional standard errors use the residuals of the fit of order p with bandwidth h, and robust bias-corrected ones those of the fit of order p + 1 with bandwidth b, both adjusted with the hat matrix of the first fit. With one observation per cluster, types 0, 1 and 2 give the standard errors of HCVariance(type), and type 3 those of HCVariance(3) times √((n - 1) / n).

Standard errors are NaN where the window holds fewer than two clusters, and for types 2 and 3 where one cluster alone determines part of the fit, so that I - H_gg is singular. For the bias-corrected estimate, nprobust adjusts the residuals with a matrix that changes with the units of x; compat = true reproduces it, and its treatment of singular blocks. The plug-in bandwidth rules estimate their variance constants in the same way; with compat = true, they use nprobust's: the residuals of HCVariance(type) with the factor c of type 1.

KernelSmoothing.LocalPolynomialRegression — Type
LocalPolynomialRegression <: RegressionModel

A local polynomial fit of a regression function or its derivative at a set of points, with conventional and robust bias-corrected inference, fitted with fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y).

At a point x₀, the fit of order p minimizes

∑ᵢ wᵢ k((xᵢ - x₀) / h) (yᵢ - β₀ - β₁ (xᵢ - x₀) - ⋯ - βₚ (xᵢ - x₀)^p)²,

and ν! β̂_ν estimates the ν-th derivative of the regression function at x₀. The bias-corrected estimate subtracts the leading bias term, estimated by a fit of order p + 1 with the pilot bandwidth b, and its standard error includes the variability of that estimate (Calonico, Cattaneo and Titiunik 2014; Calonico, Cattaneo and Farrell 2018), as in nprobust.

coef(m) and stderror(m) give the conventional estimates and standard errors at the points, confint(m; level, method) the intervals, predict(m, x) the estimates at new points, and coeftable(m) everything as a table, that is, a named tuple of columns.

KernelSmoothing.NNVariance — Type
NNVariance(matches = 3)

Nearest-neighbour variance estimates σ̂ᵢ² = Jᵢ / (Jᵢ + 1) (yᵢ - ȳᵢ)², where ȳᵢ is the mean response of the Jᵢ nearest neighbours of xᵢ among the observations in the window. The neighbours are at least matches observations, together with all observations tied with xᵢ or with the farthest neighbour (Abadie and Imbens 2006; Calonico, Cattaneo and Titiunik 2014). This is the default of nprobust and rdrobust. Neighbours on the left and on the right at the same distance are both taken, also when rounding makes the two distances differ in the last digits, as in an equally spaced design, where it changed the standard errors by up to 9 percent under a shift of the design; with compat = true, the distances are compared exactly, as in nprobust. The estimates do not depend on the fitted polynomials, so conventional and robust bias-corrected standard errors share them.

KernelSmoothing.RuleOfThumb — Type
RuleOfThumb()

The rule-of-thumb bandwidth of Fan and Gijbels (1996, Section 4.2) for local polynomial regression of order p and derivative ν,

h = C_{ν,p}(k) (σ̃² (max x - min x) / (n D))^(1/(2p+3)),

the bandwidth that minimizes the asymptotic integrated mean squared error when the variance σ̃² and the mean squared derivative D of order p + 1 come from a global polynomial fit of order p + 3, or of lower order when x has fewer than p + 4 distinct values. The constant C_{ν,p}(k) depends on the equivalent kernel, and n is the effective sample size. The global fit uses the standardized covariate, so that the rule is equivariant under changes of location and scale. When p - ν is even, the rule of order p + 1 is used, which has the same rate. The bandwidth is capped at the range of the data, which it reaches when the derivative of the global fit vanishes.

StatsAPI.fit — Method
fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y; derivative = 0, degree = derivative + 1,
    kernel = EpanechnikovKernel(), bandwidth = nothing, pilot = nothing, rho = 1,
    points = nothing, vce = nothing, minobs = 21, weights = nothing, cluster = nothing,
    compat = false)
fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, d::SmoothingData; kwargs...)

Fits local polynomials of order degree to the responses y at points, by default 30 equally spaced points between the quantiles of order 0.025 and 0.975 of x (between the smallest and the largest x with compat = true, as nprobust), and estimates the regression function or its derivative of order derivative. The default order, derivative + 1, is nprobust's: an odd degree - derivative has the same order of bias at the boundary as in the interior.

  • bandwidth is a number, a vector with one bandwidth per point, or a BandwidthSelector for regression. By default it follows nprobust: MSEOptimal at a single point and IMSEOptimal at several, with adaptive pilot bandwidths unless compat = true.
  • pilot is the bandwidth b of the fit of order degree + 1 that estimates the bias; by default b = h / rho, so that rho = 1 uses the same bandwidth for both, as nprobust does. With pilot = :selected, the fit uses the pilot bandwidths that the bandwidth selector estimated.
  • vce estimates the conditional variances: NNVariance (the default) or HCVariance, or for clustered data ClusterVariance (the default with clusters).
  • cluster gives the cluster of each observation, as identifiers of any type, for cluster-robust standard errors.
  • minobs raises h and b at each point, where needed, to the distance of the minobs-th nearest observation, so that every window holds enough data, and further where ties leave too few distinct values with positive weight: p + 1 in the window of h for the fit and p + 2 in that of b for its bias correction. minobs = 0 turns both off, and the fit records where they were binding. Estimates whose window still holds fewer distinct values than coefficients are NaN.
  • compat = true gives the plug-in rules (MSEOptimal, IMSEOptimal and CoverageOptimal) nprobust's pilot bandwidths instead of the adaptive ones, so that they agree with nprobust 1.0.0, and the floor and the nearest-neighbour variances are nprobust's: the floor counts observations only, and neighbours at distances that differ by rounding are not tied.

The estimates are exact sums over the windows; with weights, they are weighted least-squares fits.

Examples

julia> x = collect(range(0, 1; length = 200)); y = sin.(2π .* x) .+ 0.1 .* cos.(37 .* x);

julia> m = fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y; bandwidth = 0.1, points = [0.25, 0.5]);

julia> round.(coef(m); digits = 3)
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.946
 0.014

Honest intervals

Robust bias correction removes an estimate of the bias, while Honest intervals (Armstrong and Kolesár 2018, 2020) bound it. For the class of regression functions whose derivative of order $p + 1$ is bounded by $M$ near $x_0$, the worst-case bias of the linear estimate $\sum_i \ell_i y_i$ is

\[B = \frac{M}{(p+1)!} \sum_{i=1}^n |\ell_i|\, |x_i - x_0|^{p+1},\]

since the estimate reproduces polynomials of order $p$. The interval is $\hat\theta \pm \mathrm{cv}(B/\widehat{\mathrm{se}})\, \widehat{\mathrm{se}}$, where $\mathrm{cv}(t)$ is the quantile of $|N(t, 1)|$ at the level of the interval, so that it covers for every function in the class. The intervals need $M$, chosen before seeing the estimates, since adapting to unknown smoothness is impossible over such classes, and Honest has no default for it. Honest(:rot) takes $M$ from a global polynomial of order $p + 3$, the rule of thumb of Armstrong and Kolesár, which they offer as a starting point for a sensitivity analysis. FLCIOptimal chooses the bandwidth that makes the interval shortest. The plug-in bandwidths of MSEOptimal estimate the bias rather than bound it, and where it is small, as where the curvature vanishes, they are much larger than the honest intervals can afford, which then become much longer. The bandwidth that minimizes the worst-case mean squared error over the class, in contrast, gives intervals only slightly longer than the shortest ones (Armstrong and Kolesár 2020).

m = fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y; points = [0.5], bandwidth = FLCIOptimal(40))
confint(m; method = Honest(40))          # covers whenever |m''| ≤ 40
confint(m; method = Honest(:rot))        # M from a global quartic, a starting point for sensitivity analysis
worstcasebias(m, 40)
KernelSmoothing.FLCIOptimal — Type
FLCIOptimal(M; gridsize = 40, level = 0.95)

The bandwidth at each point that minimizes the length of the Honest interval with bound M and coverage level, cv(B(h) / se(h)) se(h), as Armstrong and Kolesár (2020) recommend for honest inference. M is required, as for Honest; :rot selects the rule of thumb. The length is evaluated with the standard errors of the fit's variance estimator on a grid of bandwidths spaced evenly on the log scale, from the distance of the max(minobs, 21)-th nearest observation to the range of the data, and the best one is refined by golden-section search. The lower end does not go below 21 observations, since residual-based standard errors shrink with the window: with HC0 variances and no floor, the search ended at windows of about three observations, where the intervals covered 3% of the time.

KernelSmoothing.Honest — Type
Honest(M; biascorrected = false, class = :default)

Honest confidence intervals for local polynomial regression (Armstrong and Kolesár 2018, 2020): they cover the regression function with probability at least level uniformly over the Taylor class of functions whose derivative of order p + 1 is bounded by M near the point,

estimate ± cv(B / se) se,   B = M / (p + 1)! ∑ᵢ |ℓᵢ| |xᵢ - x₀|^(p+1),

where ℓᵢ are the weights of the estimate, B is its worst-case bias over the class and cv(t) is the level quantile of |N(t, 1)|. The intervals account for the bias instead of removing it, so that they need a bound on the smoothness: adapting to unknown smoothness is impossible over such classes. The bound M is therefore required, chosen before seeing the estimates. With M = :rot, it is the largest absolute derivative of order p + 1 of a global polynomial of order p + 3 over the range of the data (of lower order when x has fewer than p + 4 distinct values), the rule of thumb of Armstrong and Kolesár for local linear fits, which they offer as a starting point for a sensitivity analysis, not as a bound. With biascorrected = true, the interval is built around the bias-corrected estimate and its weights.

For RegressionDiscontinuity, the class is by default the Hölder class of functions whose second derivative is bounded by M on each side of the cutoff, as in RDHonest, and class = :taylor gives the Taylor class; a fuzzy design takes a pair M = (M_Y, M_T) of bounds for the outcome and the treatment, and :rot the rule of thumb of RDHonest.

Uniform bands

vcov gives the covariances of the estimates at the points. Each estimate is linear in the data, and its standard error is the norm of the vector $a_j$ with entries $a_{ij} = \ell_{ij} \hat\varepsilon_{ij}$, the weight of observation $i$ at point $j$ times its residual from the variance estimator. The covariances are the inner products $a_j' a_l$, so that the matrix is positive semidefinite, its diagonal holds the squared standard errors of the fit, and estimates whose windows do not overlap are uncorrelated.

confband returns the sup-t band $\hat\theta_j \pm c\, \widehat{\mathrm{se}}_j$ of Chernozhukov, Chetverikov and Kato (2014), where $c$ is the quantile of $\max_j |Z_j|$ for a Gaussian vector $Z$ with the estimated correlations, found by simulation. It covers the curve at all points at once, for regression and density fits alike. Pointwise intervals used as a band cover far less often than their level.

m = fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y; points = range(0, 1; length = 50))
b = confband(m)              # robust bias-corrected 95% band

KernelSmoothing.confband — Method
confband(m; level = 0.95, method = RobustBiasCorrected(), draws = 10_000, rng = Random.default_rng())

A uniform confidence band over the points of a pointwise fit m (LocalPolynomialRegression or PointwiseDensity): the intervals estimate ± c se at all points, with the critical value c chosen so that they cover the curve at all points at once with probability level. As in the sup-t bands of Chernozhukov, Chetverikov and Kato (2014) and lpdensity, c is the level quantile of maxⱼ |Zⱼ| for a Gaussian vector Z with the correlations of vcov(m; method), from draws simulated draws. The critical value lies between the pointwise one and the Bonferroni one, and the simulated quantile is kept between them, since it can cross them by chance; it is close to the pointwise one when neighbouring estimates are highly correlated, and equals it at one point.

Returns a named tuple with the lower and upper ends of the band and the critical value critical. By default the band is robust bias-corrected, centred at the bias-corrected estimates; with method = Conventional() it ignores the bias.

StatsAPI.vcov — Method
vcov(m; method = Conventional())

The covariance matrix of the estimates of a pointwise fit m (LocalPolynomialRegression or PointwiseDensity) at its points, or with method = RobustBiasCorrected() of the bias-corrected estimates.

Each estimate is linear in the data, and its standard error is the norm of a vector of residual-weighted kernel weights. The covariances are the inner products of these vectors, so that the matrix is positive semidefinite and its diagonal holds the squared standard errors of the fit. Regression estimates whose windows do not overlap are uncorrelated; density estimates are then slightly negatively correlated, since every observation counts towards the total weight. For regression, these are the covariances of nprobust's covgrid with HC variances; nprobust recomputes nearest-neighbour residuals on the union of two windows, which changes them slightly near the edges. With ClusterVariance, the vectors hold sums over clusters, so that estimates whose windows share a cluster are correlated; nprobust's covgrid ignores the clusters off the diagonal.
