Local polynomial regression

fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y) fits local polynomials at a set of points and estimates the regression function or one of its derivatives, with conventional and robust bias-corrected standard errors and confidence intervals (Calonico, Cattaneo and Farrell 2018). With given bandwidths, the results agree with nprobust to rounding.

using KernelSmoothing

x = rand(2000); y = sin.(2π .* x) .+ 0.3 .* randn(2000)
m = fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y; points = range(0, 1; length = 21))
coef(m)                                  # the estimates at the points
confint(m)                               # robust bias-corrected 95% intervals
confint(m; method = Conventional())      # intervals that ignore the bias
predict(m, [0.25, 0.5])                  # estimates at new points
coeftable(m)                             # everything, as a table of columns
fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y; degree = 2, derivative = 1)   # the derivative

The estimator

Let $(x_i, y_i)$ be the observations with weights $w_i$, $K$ a kernel and $h$ a bandwidth. At a point $x_0$, the fit of order $p$ minimizes

\[\sum_{i=1}^n w_i K\Big(\frac{x_i - x_0}{h}\Big) \Big( y_i - \sum_{j=0}^p \beta_j (x_i - x_0)^j \Big)^2 ,\]

and $\nu!\, \hat\beta_\nu$ estimates the derivative $m^{(\nu)}(x_0)$ of the regression function for $0 \leq \nu \leq p$. In the scaled variable $u = (x - x_0)/h$, with $r_p(u) = (1, u, \dots, u^p)'$ and the Gram matrix $\Gamma_p = \sum_i w_i K(u_i) r_p(u_i) r_p(u_i)'$, the estimate is linear in the responses, $\sum_i \ell_i y_i$ with $\ell_i = (\nu!/h^\nu)\, w_i K(u_i)\, e_\nu' \Gamma_p^{-1} r_p(u_i)$.

The leading bias of the fit is $\Gamma_p^{-1} \Lambda\, \beta_{p+1}$, where $\Lambda = \sum_i w_i K(u_i) u_i^{p+1} r_p(u_i)$ and $\beta_{p+1}$ is the next Taylor coefficient (Calonico, Cattaneo and Titiunik 2014). We estimate $\beta_{p+1}$ by a fit of order $q = p + 1$ with the pilot bandwidth $b$, in the variable $v = (x - x_0)/b$. The bias-corrected estimate is then $\sum_i \ell_i^{\mathrm{bc}} y_i$ with

\[\ell_i^{\mathrm{bc}} = \ell_i - \frac{\nu!}{h^\nu} \Big(\frac{h}{b}\Big)^{p+1} (g' \Lambda)\, w_i K(v_i)\, c' r_q(v_i), \qquad g = \Gamma_p^{-1} e_\nu, \qquad c = \Gamma_q^{-1} e_{p+1} ,\]

where $\Gamma_q$ is the Gram matrix of the second fit. The standard errors are $(\sum_i \ell_i^2 \hat\sigma_i^2)^{1/2}$ and $(\sum_i (\ell_i^{\mathrm{bc}})^2 \hat\sigma_i^2)^{1/2}$, so that the robust one includes the variability of the bias estimate and its covariance with the estimate. By default $b = h$, as in nprobust, and then the bias-corrected estimate is the fit of order $p + 1$ at $h$.

The conditional variances $\hat\sigma_i^2$ come from NNVariance, the default, which compares each response with the mean of its nearest neighbours in the window, or from HCVariance, which uses the residuals of the local fits with the corrections HC0 to HC3. In small samples at the boundary, HC0 understates the variance more than the nearest-neighbour estimator and HC3. At each point, the bandwidths are raised where needed to the distance of the minobs-th nearest observation, 21 by default as nprobust's bwcheck, and the fit records the points where this floor binds.

The default bandwidths follow nprobust: the plug-in bandwidth MSEOptimal at a single point and IMSEOptimal at several, with pilot bandwidths anchored on cross-validation; see Bandwidth selection.

KernelSmoothing.Conventional — Type
Conventional()

Intervals estimate ± z se from the estimate and its standard error. They ignore the smoothing bias, so they cover only for bandwidths small enough that the bias is negligible, which bandwidths that minimize the mean squared error are not.

KernelSmoothing.HCVariance — Type
HCVariance(type)

Heteroskedasticity-consistent variance estimates from the residuals ε̂ᵢ of the local fits: ε̂ᵢ² for type = 0, scaled by n / (n - k) for type = 1, by 1 / (1 - hᵢᵢ) for type = 2 and by 1 / (1 - hᵢᵢ)² for type = 3. Here n is the number of observations in the window, k the number of coefficients, and hᵢᵢ the leverage of observation i in the local fit of order p. Conventional standard errors use the residuals of the fit of order p with bandwidth h, robust bias-corrected ones those of the fit of order p + 1 with bandwidth b, both with the leverages of the first fit, as in nprobust.

KernelSmoothing.LocalPolynomialRegression — Type
LocalPolynomialRegression <: RegressionModel

A local polynomial fit of a regression function or its derivative at a set of points, with conventional and robust bias-corrected inference, fitted with fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y).

At a point x₀, the fit of order p minimizes

∑ᵢ wᵢ k((xᵢ - x₀) / h) (yᵢ - β₀ - β₁ (xᵢ - x₀) - ⋯ - βₚ (xᵢ - x₀)^p)²,

and ν! β̂_ν estimates the ν-th derivative of the regression function at x₀. The bias-corrected estimate subtracts the leading bias term, estimated by a fit of order p + 1 with the pilot bandwidth b, and its standard error includes the variability of that estimate (Calonico, Cattaneo and Titiunik 2014; Calonico, Cattaneo and Farrell 2018), as in nprobust.

coef(m) and stderror(m) give the conventional estimates and standard errors at the points, confint(m; level, method) the intervals, predict(m, x) the estimates at new points, and coeftable(m) everything as a table, that is, a named tuple of columns.

KernelSmoothing.NNVariance — Type
NNVariance(matches = 3)

Nearest-neighbour variance estimates σ̂ᵢ² = Jᵢ / (Jᵢ + 1) (yᵢ - ȳᵢ)², where ȳᵢ is the mean response of the Jᵢ nearest neighbours of xᵢ among the observations in the window. The neighbours are at least matches observations, together with all observations tied with xᵢ or with the farthest neighbour (Abadie and Imbens 2006; Calonico, Cattaneo and Titiunik 2014). This is the default of nprobust and rdrobust. The estimates do not depend on the fitted polynomials, so conventional and robust bias-corrected standard errors share them.

KernelSmoothing.RobustBiasCorrected — Type
RobustBiasCorrected()

Intervals centred at the bias-corrected estimate, with a standard error that includes the variability of the bias correction (Calonico, Cattaneo and Farrell 2018). They remain valid at bandwidths that minimize the mean squared error.

KernelSmoothing.RuleOfThumb — Type
RuleOfThumb()

The rule-of-thumb bandwidth of Fan and Gijbels (1996, Section 4.2) for local polynomial regression of order p and derivative ν,

h = C_{ν,p}(k) (σ̃² (max x - min x) / (n D))^(1/(2p+3)),

the bandwidth that minimizes the asymptotic integrated mean squared error when the variance σ̃² and the mean squared derivative D of order p + 1 come from a global polynomial fit of order p + 3. The constant C_{ν,p}(k) depends on the equivalent kernel, and n is the effective sample size. The global fit uses the standardized covariate, so that the rule is equivariant under changes of location and scale. When p - ν is even, the rule of order p + 1 is used, which has the same rate. The bandwidth is capped at the range of the data.

StatsAPI.fit — Method
fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y; degree = 1, derivative = 0, kernel = EpanechnikovKernel(),
    bandwidth = nothing, pilot = nothing, rho = 1, points = nothing,
    vce = NNVariance(), minobs = 21, weights = nothing, compat = false)
fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, d::SmoothingData; kwargs...)

Fits local polynomials of order degree to the responses y at points, by default 30 equally spaced points between the smallest and the largest x, and estimates the regression function or its derivative of order derivative.

  • bandwidth is a number, a vector with one bandwidth per point, or a BandwidthSelector for regression. By default it follows nprobust: MSEOptimal at a single point and IMSEOptimal at several, with adaptive pilot bandwidths unless compat = true.
  • pilot is the bandwidth b of the fit of order degree + 1 that estimates the bias; by default b = h / rho, so that rho = 1 uses the same bandwidth for both, as nprobust does. With pilot = :selected, the fit uses the pilot bandwidths that the bandwidth selector estimated.
  • vce estimates the conditional variances: NNVariance (default) or HCVariance.
  • minobs raises h and b at each point, where needed, to the distance of the minobs-th nearest observation, so that every window holds enough data; minobs = 0 turns this off. The fit records where the floor was binding.
  • compat = true gives the plug-in rules (MSEOptimal, IMSEOptimal and CoverageOptimal) nprobust's pilot bandwidths instead of the adaptive ones, so that they agree with nprobust 1.0.0.

The estimates are exact sums over the windows; with weights, they are weighted least-squares fits.

Examples

julia> x = collect(range(0, 1; length = 200)); y = sin.(2π .* x) .+ 0.1 .* cos.(37 .* x);

julia> m = fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y; bandwidth = 0.1, points = [0.25, 0.5]);

julia> round.(coef(m); digits = 3)
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.946
 0.014

Honest intervals

Robust bias correction removes an estimate of the bias, while Honest intervals (Armstrong and Kolesár 2018, 2020) bound it. For the class of regression functions whose derivative of order $p + 1$ is bounded by $M$ near $x_0$, the worst-case bias of the linear estimate $\sum_i \ell_i y_i$ is

\[B = \frac{M}{(p+1)!} \sum_{i=1}^n |\ell_i|\, |x_i - x_0|^{p+1},\]

since the estimate reproduces polynomials of order $p$. The interval is $\hat\theta \pm \mathrm{cv}(B/\widehat{\mathrm{se}})\, \widehat{\mathrm{se}}$, where $\mathrm{cv}(t)$ is the quantile of $|N(t, 1)|$ at the level of the interval, so that it covers for every function in the class. The intervals need $M$, since adapting to unknown smoothness is impossible over such classes. Honest() takes $M$ from a global polynomial of order $p + 3$, the rule of thumb of Armstrong and Kolesár, which they offer as a starting point for a sensitivity analysis. FLCIOptimal chooses the bandwidth that makes the interval shortest, which honest intervals need: at the MSE-optimal bandwidths they are much longer.

m = fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y; points = [0.5], bandwidth = FLCIOptimal(40))
confint(m; method = Honest(40))          # covers whenever |m''| ≤ 40
confint(m; method = Honest())            # M from a global quartic, a starting point for sensitivity analysis
worstcasebias(m, 40)
KernelSmoothing.FLCIOptimal — Type
FLCIOptimal(M = :rot; gridsize = 40)

The bandwidth at each point that minimizes the length of the Honest interval with bound M, cv(B(h) / se(h)) se(h), as Armstrong and Kolesár (2020) recommend for honest inference. The length is evaluated with the standard errors of the fit's variance estimator on a grid of bandwidths spaced evenly on the log scale, from the distance of the minobs-th nearest observation to the range of the data, and the best one is refined by golden-section search.

KernelSmoothing.Honest — Type
Honest(M = :rot; biascorrected = false)

Honest confidence intervals for local polynomial regression (Armstrong and Kolesár 2018, 2020): they cover the regression function with probability at least level uniformly over the Taylor class of functions whose derivative of order p + 1 is bounded by M near the point,

estimate ± cv(B / se) se,   B = M / (p + 1)! ∑ᵢ |ℓᵢ| |xᵢ - x₀|^(p+1),

where ℓᵢ are the weights of the estimate, B is its worst-case bias over the class and cv(t) is the level quantile of |N(t, 1)|. The intervals account for the bias instead of removing it, so that they need a bound on the smoothness: adapting to unknown smoothness is impossible over such classes. With M = :rot, the bound is the largest absolute derivative of order p + 1 of a global polynomial of order p + 3 over the range of the data, the rule of thumb of Armstrong and Kolesár for local linear fits, which they offer as a starting point for sensitivity analysis. With biascorrected = true, the interval is built around the bias-corrected estimate and its weights.

Uniform bands

vcov gives the covariances of the estimates at the points. Each estimate is linear in the data, and its standard error is the norm of the vector $a_j$ with entries $a_{ij} = \ell_{ij} \hat\varepsilon_{ij}$, the weight of observation $i$ at point $j$ times its residual from the variance estimator. The covariances are the inner products $a_j' a_l$, so that the matrix is positive semidefinite, its diagonal holds the squared standard errors of the fit, and estimates whose windows do not overlap are uncorrelated.

confband returns the sup-t band $\hat\theta_j \pm c\, \widehat{\mathrm{se}}_j$ of Chernozhukov, Chetverikov and Kato (2014), where $c$ is the quantile of $\max_j |Z_j|$ for a Gaussian vector $Z$ with the estimated correlations, found by simulation. It covers the curve at all points at once, for regression and density fits alike. Pointwise intervals used as a band cover far less often than their level.

m = fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y; points = range(0, 1; length = 50))
b = confband(m)              # robust bias-corrected 95% band
b.lower, b.upper, b.critical

KernelSmoothing.confband — Method
confband(m; level = 0.95, method = RobustBiasCorrected(), draws = 10_000, rng = Random.default_rng())

A uniform confidence band over the points of a pointwise fit m (LocalPolynomialRegression or PointwiseDensity): the intervals estimate ± c se at all points, with the critical value c chosen so that they cover the curve at all points at once with probability level. As in the sup-t bands of Chernozhukov, Chetverikov and Kato (2014) and lpdensity, c is the level quantile of maxⱼ |Zⱼ| for a Gaussian vector Z with the correlations of vcov(m; method), from draws simulated draws. The critical value lies between the pointwise one and the Bonferroni one, and is close to the pointwise one when neighbouring estimates are highly correlated.

Returns a named tuple with the lower and upper ends of the band and the critical value critical. By default the band is robust bias-corrected, centred at the bias-corrected estimates; with method = Conventional() it ignores the bias.

source
StatsAPI.vcov — Method
vcov(m; method = Conventional())

The covariance matrix of the estimates of a pointwise fit m (LocalPolynomialRegression or PointwiseDensity) at its points, or with method = RobustBiasCorrected() of the bias-corrected estimates.

Each estimate is linear in the data, and its standard error is the norm of a vector of residual-weighted kernel weights. The covariances are the inner products of these vectors, so that the matrix is positive semidefinite and its diagonal holds the squared standard errors of the fit. Estimates whose windows do not overlap are uncorrelated. For regression, these are the covariances of nprobust's covgrid with HC variances; nprobust recomputes nearest-neighbour residuals on the union of two windows, which changes them slightly near the edges.
