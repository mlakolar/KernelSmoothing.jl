Regression discontinuity

fit(RegressionDiscontinuity, x, y; cutoff) estimates the effect of a treatment assigned by whether a running variable reaches a cutoff, from local polynomials on the two sides of the cutoff, with conventional and robust bias-corrected inference (Calonico, Cattaneo and Titiunik 2014). It covers sharp, fuzzy and kink designs and adjustment for covariates, and selects its bandwidths by the plug-in rules of rdrobust. The estimates, standard errors and bandwidths agree with rdrobust to rounding. Honest intervals and their bandwidths are those of RDHonest, and fit(DensityDiscontinuity, x; cutoff) gives the manipulation test of rddensity. Designs with several cutoffs, power calculations and inference under local randomization follow rdmulti, rdpower and rdlocrand.

using KernelSmoothing

x = 2 .* rand(2000) .- 1
y = 0.5 .+ x .+ 0.4 .* (x .>= 0) .+ 0.3 .* randn(2000)
m = fit(RegressionDiscontinuity, x, y; cutoff = 0)
coef(m)                                  # the estimated jump
confint(m)                               # the robust bias-corrected 95% interval
coeftable(m)                             # conventional, bias-corrected and robust results
bandwidth(m)                             # the MSE-optimal bandwidths on the two sides
fit(RegressionDiscontinuity, x, y; derivative = 1)            # the jump in the slope
fit(RegressionDiscontinuity, x, y; treatment = t)             # a fuzzy design
fit(RegressionDiscontinuity, x, y; covariates = Z)            # adjusted for covariates
fit(RegressionDiscontinuity, x, y; bandwidth = RDOptimal(:cerrd))   # coverage-error rate
fit(RegressionDiscontinuity, x, y; bandwidth = 0.3)           # a given bandwidth

The estimator

The observations with $x_i < c$ form the left side and the others the right side. On each side, the local polynomial fit of order $p$ at $c$ with bandwidth $h$ and the fit of order $p + 1$ with the pilot bandwidth $b$ are those of Local polynomial regression, with the data of that side only, so that they extrapolate to the cutoff from one side. Let $\hat\beta_{+,\nu}$ and $\hat\beta_{-,\nu}$ be the coefficients of $(x - c)^\nu$ on the right and on the left. In a sharp design, the estimate is

\[\hat\tau = \nu! \, (\hat\beta_{+,\nu} - \hat\beta_{-,\nu}) ,\]

the jump in the regression function for derivative = 0 and the jump in its slope for derivative = 1, a kink design. The bias-corrected estimate subtracts the estimated bias on each side, and its robust standard error includes the variability of that estimate. The two sides are independent, so that the variances add.

In a fuzzy design, the treatment received is passed as treatment, and the estimate is the ratio $\hat\tau_Y / \hat\tau_T$ of the jumps in the outcome and in the treatment. Its standard error comes from the delta method: it is the standard error of the estimate for the combination $(Y - \hat\tau T) / \hat\tau_T$ of the outcome and the treatment, and m.firststage holds the jump in the treatment with its standard errors.

With covariates, the outcome is adjusted by $Z'\hat\gamma$, where the coefficients $\hat\gamma$ are common to both sides and come from the weighted least-squares fit of the outcome on the covariates and the local polynomials of the two sides (Calonico, Cattaneo, Farrell and Titiunik 2019). Collinear covariates are dropped. In a fuzzy design, the treatment is adjusted in the same way.

The variances come from the same estimators as for local polynomial regression: NNVariance (the default), HCVariance, and for clustered data, given as cluster, ClusterVariance (CR1 by default). As in rdrobust, the residuals of the bias correction are adjusted with the leverages of the fits of order $p + 1$ that produce them, for HC2, HC3, CR2 and CR3; for local polynomial regression, nprobust adjusts them with the leverages of the fits of order $p$.

Two details depart from rdrobust, which compat = true reproduces. First, where a leverage is one, the standard error is NaN, while rdrobust raises $1 - h_{ii}$ to $10^{-8}$, and the same holds for singular blocks of clusters. Second, ClusterVariance(3) has the factor $(G - 1)/G$, as in nprobust, which rdrobust leaves out.

Kink designs

In a regression kink design (Card, Lee, Pei and Weber 2015), the treatment is a continuous function of the running variable whose slope changes at the cutoff, as a benefit that is a fixed fraction of earnings up to a maximum. The regression function is then continuous at the cutoff, and the effect of the treatment shows up as a change in its slope: with derivative = 1, the estimate is the jump in the first derivative, $\hat\beta_{+,1} - \hat\beta_{-,1}$, from local polynomials of order degree = 2 by default (order $p = \nu + 1$, as for derivatives in local polynomial regression), and the bias correction uses fits of order 3. In a fuzzy kink design, the treatment received is not a deterministic function of the running variable, and the estimate is the ratio of the jumps in the slopes of the outcome and of the treatment,

\[\hat\tau = \frac{\hat\beta^Y_{+,1} - \hat\beta^Y_{-,1}}{\hat\beta^T_{+,1} - \hat\beta^T_{-,1}},\]

the effect of the treatment for the units at the cutoff, under the conditions of Card, Lee, Pei and Weber (2015). The standard errors and the bandwidth selectors are those of the jump, applied to the derivative: RDOptimal chooses bandwidths that are optimal for the estimate of the jump in the slope, with the constants of the derivative of order one. Kink estimates need many more observations than jumps, since the derivative converges at a slower rate.

x = 2 .* rand(4000) .- 1
t = 0.5 .* x .- 0.4 .* max.(x, 0) .+ 0.1 .* randn(4000)   # the slope of E(t | x) changes at 0
y = 1 .+ 1.5 .* t .+ 0.2 .* x .^ 2 .+ 0.3 .* randn(4000)
fit(RegressionDiscontinuity, x, y; derivative = 1)               # the kink in y: 1.5 × (-0.4)
fit(RegressionDiscontinuity, x, y; derivative = 1, treatment = t) # fuzzy kink: the effect 1.5

Bandwidths

By default the bandwidths come from RDOptimal(:mserd), the bandwidth common to both sides that minimizes the asymptotic mean squared error of the estimate of the jump, with the pilot bandwidth $b$ selected in the same way for the bias correction. The other rules of rdrobust select a bandwidth for each side or for the sum of the estimates, combine these, or shrink them to the rate that minimizes the coverage error of the robust intervals. select_bandwidth(RDOptimal(), x, y; cutoff) returns the bandwidths without the fit, as rdrobust's rdbwselect.

The rules estimate the variance and the bias of the local fits with pilot fits, as the plug-in rules of local polynomial regression do, after standardizing $x$ and $y$. Where 20 percent or more of the observations on a side repeat values of $x$, masspoints = :adjust (the default) counts distinct values rather than observations and keeps at least 10 distinct values in every window. A bandwidth can also be given, as one number or a pair for the left and the right side, with pilot (by default $b = h$) or rho ($b = h / \rho$).

Several cutoffs

In a design with several cutoffs (Cattaneo, Keele, Titiunik and Vazquez-Bare 2016), different units face different cutoffs, as when a program's eligibility threshold varies by region or year. fit(MultipleCutoffs, x, y, c), with c the cutoff that each unit faces, estimates the effect at each cutoff from its units, their weighted average with weights proportional to the observations in the windows, and the pooled effect, from the running variable normalized to $x - c$; the pooled estimand is an average of the effects at the cutoffs weighted by the densities of the running variable there. With cumulative cutoffs, one running variable crosses several thresholds at which the treatment changes, and fit(CumulativeCutoffs, x, y; cutoffs) estimates the effect at each, optionally from the observations in a range around it. Both reproduce rdmulti's rdmc and rdms.

fit(MultipleCutoffs, x, y, c)                          # cutoffs per unit
fit(CumulativeCutoffs, x, y; cutoffs = [33, 66])        # thresholds of one score

Power and sample size

rd_power gives the power of the robust bias-corrected test of no effect against an effect $\tau$ (Cattaneo, Titiunik and Vazquez-Bare 2019),

\[1 - \Phi\Big(z_{1-\alpha/2} - \frac{\tau}{\widehat{\mathrm{se}}}\Big) + \Phi\Big(-z_{1-\alpha/2} - \frac{\tau}{\widehat{\mathrm{se}}}\Big),\]

with the robust standard error of a fit, for the sample at hand or for a sample of $n$ observations spread in the same way, whose standard error is $\widehat{\mathrm{se}} \sqrt{N/n}$ at the same bandwidths. rd_samplesize is the smallest $n$ that reaches a given power. Both agree with rdpower's rdpower and rdsampsi, which plan a study from a pilot sample or a previous one.

m = fit(RegressionDiscontinuity, x, y)
rd_power(m; tau = 0.3)                  # the power against an effect of 0.3
rd_samplesize(m; tau = 0.3, power = 0.8) # the observations needed for 80%

Local randomization

Under local randomization (Cattaneo, Frandsen and Titiunik 2015), the treatment is taken to be assigned as if at random in a small window $[w_l, w_r]$ around the cutoff, so that the units above and below the cutoff are comparable without a model for the regression function. fit(LocalRandomization, x, y; window) tests the sharp null hypothesis that the treatment changes every outcome in the window by $\tau_0$ (nulltau, by default zero) by randomization inference: the treatment labels are permuted within the window, with the number treated fixed, and the p-value is the fraction of permutations whose statistic is at least as large in absolute value as the observed one. The statistics are the difference in means, the Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic and the standardized rank sum, and each also has an asymptotic p-value: from the normal distribution for the first and the last, and for the Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic the exact one given the ties when $n_0 n_1 < 10000$. The statistics and the asymptotic p-values agree with rdlocrand's rdrandinf to rounding, and the randomization p-values within their Monte Carlo error. The window is given; its choice by balance tests on covariates, as rdwinselect, is not implemented.

m = fit(LocalRandomization, x, y; window = 0.2, statistic = :all, reps = 10_000)
coeftable(m)                             # the statistics and their p-values
fit(LocalRandomization, x, y; window = (-0.1, 0.3), nulltau = 0.5)

Honest intervals

Robust bias correction estimates the bias and removes it, while honest intervals (Armstrong and Kolesár 2018, 2020) bound it over a class of regression functions, as RDHonest. Over the Hölder class of functions whose second derivative is bounded by $M$ on each side of the cutoff, the worst-case bias of a local linear estimate with weights $\ell_i$ is

\[\bar b = \frac{M}{2} \Big| \sum_i \ell_i (x_i - c)^2 \Big| ,\]

since the weights of a local linear estimate at the boundary have the same sign on each side, and the interval is $\hat\tau \pm \mathrm{cv}(\bar b / \mathrm{se}) \, \mathrm{se}$, where $\mathrm{cv}(t)$ is the quantile of $|N(t, 1)|$. class = :taylor gives the Taylor class instead. In a fuzzy design, $M = (M_Y, M_T)$ bounds the outcome and the treatment, and the bias is that of $(M_Y + M_T |\hat\tau|) / |\hat f|$ for the jump $\hat f$ in the treatment. M = :rot takes the rule of thumb of RDHonest, the largest second derivative of global quartic fits on the two sides, which Armstrong and Kolesár offer as a starting point for a sensitivity analysis rather than as a bound.

RDHonestOptimal(M) chooses the bandwidth that minimizes the worst-case mean squared error, the length of the honest interval or the excess length of a one-sided interval, from preliminary variances that are constant on each side, as RDHonest. With these bandwidths and its standard errors, nearest-neighbour or $\mathrm{HC0}$ (RDHonest's "EHW"), the intervals agree with RDHonest to rounding.

m = fit(RegressionDiscontinuity, x, y; bandwidth = RDHonestOptimal(1.5))
confint(m; method = Honest(1.5))                       # the honest 95% interval
worst_case_bias(m, 1.5)                                  # its bound on the bias
m = fit(RegressionDiscontinuity, x, y; treatment = t, bandwidth = RDHonestOptimal((1.5, 0.5); criterion = :flci))
confint(m; method = Honest((1.5, 0.5)))

The manipulation test

A regression discontinuity design assumes that units cannot sort themselves around the cutoff, which would make the density of the running variable jump there. fit(DensityDiscontinuity, x; cutoff) gives the test of Cattaneo, Jansson and Ma (2020), as rddensity: local polynomial estimates of the density on the two sides of the cutoff and the test that they are equal.

m = fit(DensityDiscontinuity, x; cutoff = 0)
m.pvalue                                 # the test of continuity of the density
coeftable(m)                             # the densities on the two sides and the jump
m.binomial                               # binomial tests in windows around the cutoff

On each side, the density is the slope at the cutoff of the local polynomial fit to the empirical distribution function, as for LocalPolynomialDensity, with separate polynomials on the two sides, or with restricted = true a distribution function that is continuous at the cutoff and has common coefficients of order two and higher. The test uses the fits of order degree + 1 at bandwidths that minimize the mean squared error of the fits of order degree, so that their bias does not distort its size, and the jackknife or plug-in standard errors of rddensity. RDDensityOptimal chooses the bandwidths as rddensity's rdbwdensity. With ties in $x$, tied observations share the value of the empirical distribution function. The binomial tests compare the numbers of observations on the two sides in windows that grow from the smallest one with 20 observations on each side up to the bandwidths. The results agree with rddensity to rounding.

KernelSmoothing.RegressionDiscontinuity — Type
RegressionDiscontinuity <: RegressionModel

A local polynomial estimate of the effect of a treatment assigned by whether a running variable x reaches a cutoff c, with conventional and robust bias-corrected inference (Calonico, Cattaneo and Titiunik 2014), as rdrobust; fitted with fit(RegressionDiscontinuity, x, y; cutoff).

On each side of the cutoff, local polynomials of order p with bandwidth h estimate the regression function and its derivatives at c, and fits of order p + 1 with the pilot bandwidth b estimate their bias, as for LocalPolynomialRegression. The estimate is the jump at c in the derivative of order derivative:

  • in a sharp design, ν! (β̂₊ − β̂₋) for the coefficients β̂₊ and β̂₋ of (x − c)^ν on the right and on the left, the jump in the regression function for derivative = 0 and in its slope (a kink design) for derivative = 1;
  • in a fuzzy design, given the treatment received as treatment, the ratio of the jumps in the outcome and in the treatment;
  • with covariates, after adjusting the outcome (and the treatment) for the covariates with coefficients common to both sides (Calonico, Cattaneo, Farrell and Titiunik 2019).

The bandwidths come from RDOptimal unless they are given. coef(m) and stderror(m) give the estimate and its standard error, with method = RobustBiasCorrected() the bias-corrected estimate and its robust standard error, confint(m; level, method) the interval (robust bias-corrected by default), and coeftable(m) the conventional, bias-corrected and robust results, as rdrobust reports them. For a fuzzy design, m.firststage holds the jump in the treatment.

StatsAPI.fit — Method
fit(RegressionDiscontinuity, x, y; cutoff = 0, bandwidth = RDOptimal(), pilot = nothing,
    rho = nothing, derivative = 0, degree = derivative + 1, kernel = TriangularKernel(),
    treatment = nothing, covariates = nothing, vce = nothing, weights = nothing,
    cluster = nothing, masspoints = :adjust, bwcheck = nothing, compat = false)

Estimates the jump at cutoff in the regression of y on the running variable x, or in its derivative of order derivative, from local polynomials of order degree on the two sides, as rdrobust. Observations with x < cutoff form the left side, the others the right.

  • bandwidth is a number, a pair of numbers for the left and the right side, or an RDOptimal rule, by default RDOptimal(:mserd), as rdrobust.
  • pilot is the bandwidth b of the fits of order degree + 1 that estimate the bias, a number or a pair. By default it is the pilot bandwidth of the rule, or b = h for a given bandwidth; with rho, b = h / rho.
  • masspoints and bwcheck are rdrobust's options for the rule: where 20 percent or more of the observations on a side repeat values of x, masspoints = :adjust (the default) counts distinct values instead of observations in the variance bandwidth and keeps 10 distinct values in every window, as bwcheck = 10 does; :check only detects mass points and :off ignores them.
  • treatment gives the treatment received, for a fuzzy design.
  • covariates is a vector or a matrix with one row per observation, for the covariate-adjusted estimate; collinear covariates are dropped.
  • vce estimates the conditional variances: NNVariance (the default), HCVariance, or for clustered data ClusterVariance (the default with clusters). As in rdrobust, the residuals of the bias correction are adjusted with the leverages of the fits of order degree + 1.
  • compat = true reproduces rdrobust's treatment of leverages near one, its factor of one for CR3, and its standard errors for clusters with singular blocks.

KernelSmoothing.RDOptimal — Type
RDOptimal(bwselect = :mserd; regularization = 1)

The plug-in bandwidths of rdrobust's rdbwselect for RegressionDiscontinuity (Calonico, Cattaneo and Titiunik 2014; Calonico, Cattaneo and Farrell 2020), named as there:

  • :mserd, the default: one bandwidth for both sides that minimizes the asymptotic mean squared error of the estimate of the jump;
  • :msetwo: a bandwidth for each side, minimizing the mean squared errors of the estimates on the two sides;
  • :msesum: one bandwidth that minimizes the mean squared error of the sum of the estimates on the two sides;
  • :msecomb1: the smaller of :mserd and :msesum;
  • :msecomb2: on each side, the median of :mserd, :msesum and :msetwo;
  • :cerrd, :certwo, :cersum, :cercomb1 and :cercomb2: these bandwidths times n^(-p / ((p + 3)(2p + 3))), with n the number of observations or, for clustered data, of clusters, which shrinks them towards the bandwidths that minimize the coverage error of the robust intervals (Calonico, Cattaneo and Farrell 2020).

Each bandwidth h comes with a pilot bandwidth b for the bias correction, selected in the same way for the derivative of order p + 1. The bias constants are estimated by fits of order p + 2 with bandwidths from a first stage, and the variance constants by fits with a normal-reference bandwidth; regularization scales the term that keeps the denominators away from zero where the estimated bias nearly vanishes, as rdrobust's scaleregul.

KernelSmoothing.RDHonestOptimal — Type
RDHonestOptimal(M = :rot; criterion = :mse, level = 0.95, power = 0.8, class = :holder, T0 = 0)

The bandwidth of RDHonest for RegressionDiscontinuity: the bandwidth, common to both sides, that minimizes the worst-case mean squared error of the local linear estimate over the class of regression functions with second derivative bounded by M (criterion = :mse, the default), the length of the honest interval (:flci), or the excess length of the one-sided interval at quantile power (:oci). KernelSmoothing.worst_case_bias — Method
worst_case_bias(m::RegressionDiscontinuity, M; class = :holder)

The worst-case bias of the estimate of a RegressionDiscontinuity fit, as in Honest: over the Hölder class of functions whose second derivative is bounded by M on each side of the cutoff, M/2 |∑ᵢ ℓᵢ (xᵢ - c)²| for the weights ℓᵢ of a local linear estimate, which have the same sign on each side; or over the Taylor class, M / (p + 1)! ∑ᵢ |ℓᵢ| |xᵢ - c|^(p+1). KernelSmoothing.DensityDiscontinuity — Type
DensityDiscontinuity <: StatisticalModel

The manipulation test of Cattaneo, Jansson and Ma (2020) for a regression discontinuity design, as rddensity: local polynomial estimates of the density of the running variable on the two sides of the cutoff, fitted with fit(DensityDiscontinuity, x; cutoff), and the test that the density is continuous at the cutoff. A density that jumps there suggests that units sort around the cutoff.

The density on each side is the slope at the cutoff of a local polynomial fit to the empirical distribution function, as for LocalPolynomialDensity, with separate polynomials on the two sides (or, with restricted = true, a distribution function that is continuous at the cutoff and has common coefficients of order two and higher). The test uses the fits of order degree + 1 at bandwidths chosen for order degree, which makes it robust to the bias, and its standard error comes from the jackknife or from the plug-in formula.

coef(m) gives the estimated jump f̂₊ - f̂₋, stderror(m) its standard error, m.statistic and m.pvalue the test, coeftable(m) the estimates on both sides, and m.binomial the exact binomial tests of the numbers of observations on the two sides in windows around the cutoff, as rddensity reports them.

KernelSmoothing.rd_power — Method
rd_power(m::RegressionDiscontinuity; tau, alpha = 0.05, n = nobs(m))

The power of the robust bias-corrected test of no effect at level alpha against the effect tau (Cattaneo, Titiunik and Vazquez-Bare 2019), as rdpower's power.rbc:

1 - Φ(z - τ/se) + Φ(-z - τ/se),   z = Φ⁻¹(1 - α/2),

where se is the robust standard error of the fit m, the standard deviation of the bias-corrected estimate. With n, the power for a sample of n observations spread as in the data, analysed with the same bandwidths, whose standard error is se √(nobs(m)/n).

KernelSmoothing.LocalRandomization — Type
LocalRandomization

Randomization inference for a regression discontinuity design under local randomization (Cattaneo, Frandsen and Titiunik 2015), as rdlocrand's rdrandinf: in a window [wl, wr] around the cutoff, where treatment is taken to be assigned as if at random, the observations above the cutoff are compared with those below by a test statistic, whose distribution under the sharp null hypothesis Yᵢ(1) - Yᵢ(0) = τ₀ for every unit comes from permuting the treatment labels within the window, with the number treated fixed. Fitted with fit(LocalRandomization, x, y; window).

m.statistic names the statistics, m.observed holds their values, m.pvalue the randomization p-values and m.asymptotic the asymptotic p-values (for the Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic in small samples, its exact p-value); coeftable(m) reports them.

StatsAPI.fit — Method
fit(LocalRandomization, x, y; window, cutoff = 0, statistic = :diffmeans, nulltau = 0,
    reps = 1000, rng = Random.default_rng())

Randomization inference in the window, a pair (wl, wr) with wl < cutoff ≤ wr or a number w for (cutoff - w, cutoff + w), for the sharp null hypothesis that the treatment changes every outcome by nulltau; the observations with x ≥ cutoff are treated. The statistic is

  • :diffmeans, the difference in means ȳ₁ - ȳ₀, with the asymptotic p-value of (ȳ₁ - ȳ₀) / √(s₁²/n₁ + s₀²/n₀) against the normal distribution;
  • :ksmirnov, the two-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic, with its exact p-value when n₀ n₁ < 10000, given the ties if there are any, and the limiting one otherwise, as R's ks.test;
  • :ranksum, the standardized rank sum of the observations below the cutoff, (W₀ - n₀(n + 1)/2) / √(n₀ n₁ (n + 1)/12), against the normal distribution;
  • :all, the three.

The randomization p-value is the fraction of the reps random permutations of the labels whose statistic is at least as large in absolute value as the observed one, drawn with rng. m.means and m.sds hold the means and standard deviations of the outcomes below and above the cutoff in the window.
