Shape constraints

Many applications know the sign of a derivative of the function they estimate: a dose response that increases, a hazard that is convex, a distribution function. A kernel estimate need not respect it, and the wiggles that violate the shape are noise that the bandwidth alone cannot remove without adding bias. Two operations impose the shape on the estimates of any fit at its points.

Rearrangement

The increasing rearrangement of Chernozhukov, Fernández-Val and Galichon (2009) sorts the estimates $\hat f(t_1), \dots, \hat f(t_m)$ at sorted points in increasing order. For an increasing function $f$, the rearranged estimate $\hat f^*$ is never farther from it than $\hat f$,

\[\Big(\sum_k |\hat f^*(t_k) - f(t_k)|^p\Big)^{1/p} \le \Big(\sum_k |\hat f(t_k) - f(t_k)|^p\Big)^{1/p} \quad \text{for every } p \ge 1,\]

a bound on the distance over all points, not at each point. The rearrangement is with respect to the counting measure on the points: over an interval for equally spaced points, as the default points of local polynomial regression, and with respect to the distribution of the data for the quantiles that are the default points of the density fits. Rearranging the lower and the upper ends of a confidence band separately gives a band that covers an increasing function at the points whenever the original band does (their Proposition 2), and the $L^p$ norm of its width over the points does not increase. rearrange(m) works for every fit with one estimate per point, and confint and conf_band of the result rearrange the intervals and bands of the fit.

Projection onto a cone of shapes

constrain(m, shape) projects the estimates onto the sequences of a shape in the weighted least-squares norm,

\[\hat\theta = \arg\min_{\theta \in \mathcal{C}} \sum_{k=1}^m v_k (\theta_k - \hat f(t_k))^2,\]

where $\mathcal{C}$ is the cone of increasing or decreasing sequences, of convex or concave ones (nonnegative or nonpositive second divided differences), or of monotone convex or concave ones: smoothing followed by an isotonic or convex regression, as Mammen (1991) proposed for monotone regression. Since $\mathcal{C}$ is a closed convex cone, the projection is never farther from a sequence of the shape than the estimates. Every sequence of a shape is a linear part plus a nonnegative combination of generators, such as the steps $1\{t \ge t_j\}$ for increasing sequences and the hinges $(t - t_j)_+$ for convex ones, so that the projection is a nonnegative least-squares problem, which the active-set method of Lawson and Hanson (1974) solves exactly. shape_projection(values, points; shape, weights) is the projection itself.

Example

using KernelSmoothing

x = rand(500); y = x .^ 2 .+ 0.2 .* randn(500)
m = fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y)
r = rearrange(m)                     # increasing estimates
coef(r), conf_band(r)                # the rearranged band
c = constrain(m, :increasing_convex) # the projection onto increasing convex sequences
coef(c)

Reference

KernelSmoothing.rearrange — Function
rearrange(m; decreasing = false)

The increasing rearrangement of Chernozhukov, Fernández-Val and Galichon (2009) of the estimates of a fit m at its points, which must be sorted: the estimates sorted in increasing order, or in decreasing order with decreasing = true. For a monotone function, the rearranged estimate is never farther from it than the original one in the $L^p$ distance over the points, for every $p ≥ 1$; the bound is on the distance over all points, not at each point. The rearrangement counts each point equally: for the equally spaced default points of LocalPolynomialRegression it is the rearrangement over an interval, and for the quantiles that are the default points of the density fits, with respect to the distribution of the data.

confint(r; kwargs...) and conf_band(r; kwargs...) rearrange the lower and upper ends of the intervals and bands of m separately, over the points where neither end is NaN. A band that covers a monotone function at the points still covers it after the rearrangement (Chernozhukov, Fernández-Val and Galichon 2009, Proposition 2), and the $L^p$ norm of its width over the points does not increase; pointwise intervals keep no such guarantee. Works for any fit whose coef holds one estimate per point, such as LocalPolynomialRegression, PointwiseDensity, LocalPolynomialDensity and LocalLikelihood, for which the estimates are those of the linear predictor (which the rearrangement of the mean equals, since the link is monotone) and which has no bands.

KernelSmoothing.constrain — Function
constrain(m, shape; weights = nothing)

The estimates of a fit m at its points projected onto the sequences of the given shape by shape_projection: smoothing followed by an isotonic or convex regression of the smooth estimate, as Mammen (1991) proposed for monotone regression. The projection never increases the distance to a function of that shape, measured over the points in the weighted norm, and removes the wiggles that violate the shape; weights (equal by default) weigh the points, and the inverse squared standard errors are a natural choice. Repeated points share one value. coef(c) returns the constrained estimates. For LocalLikelihood these are the estimates of the linear predictor, so that a convex shape is convexity of the logit or the logarithm of the mean. For intervals that respect a monotone shape, see rearrange.

KernelSmoothing.shape_projection — Function
shape_projection(values, points; shape = :increasing, weights = nothing)

The weighted least-squares projection of values at the sorted points onto the cone of the sequences of the given shape,

θ̂ = argmin ∑ₖ vₖ (θₖ - valuesₖ)²   over the θ of that shape,

with v = weights (by default equal): :increasing or :decreasing (isotonic regression), :convex or :concave (nonnegative or nonpositive second divided differences at the points), or a monotone convex or concave shape such as :increasing_concave. The projection is exact: θ is written in the generators of the cone, such as the steps 1{t ≥ tⱼ} for increasing sequences and the hinges (t - tⱼ)₊ for convex ones, plus a linear part, and the nonnegative coefficients of the generators solve a nonnegative least-squares problem by the active-set method of Lawson and Hanson (1974). The cones do not change under increasing affine maps of the points, and the projection is equivariant under the scale of the values. Its cost grows with the number of generators in the solution: a few milliseconds for a smooth estimate at 1000 points, but for values that already have the shape, every generator enters, and the projection takes about 0.1 s at 200 points, 1 s at 400 and 15 s at 800.
