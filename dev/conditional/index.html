Conditional densities and quantiles

Conditional density estimation

For observations $(x_i, y_i)$ with weights $w_i$, the kernel estimate of the conditional density of $y$ given $x$ smooths the kernels of the responses as a local polynomial regression smooths the responses:

\[\hat f(y \mid x) = \sum_{i=1}^n \ell_i(x)\, \frac{1}{b} K\Big(\frac{y - y_i}{b}\Big),\]

where $K$ is the kernel in $y$ with bandwidth $b$ and $\ell_i(x)$ are the weights of the local polynomial fit of order $p$ at $x$ with kernel $k$ and bandwidth $h$. For $p = 0$ (Rosenblatt 1969; Hyndman, Bashtannyk and Grunwald 1996),

\[\ell_i(x) = \frac{w_i\, k((x_i - x)/h)}{\sum_j w_j\, k((x_j - x)/h)},\]

which are nonnegative and sum to one, so that $\hat f(\cdot \mid x)$ is a density: conditional(m, x) returns it as a KDE of the responses with these weights, with its distribution function and quantiles. For $p = 1$ (Fan, Yao and Tong 1996), the estimate has a smaller bias near the ends of the range of $x$, but the weights can be negative.

ConditionalCV chooses $(h, b)$ for $p = 0$ by maximizing the leave-one-out log-likelihood $\sum_i w_i \log \hat f_{-i}(y_i \mid x_i)$, the default of the R package np, or by minimizing the least-squares criterion of Hall, Racine and Li (2004),

\[\mathrm{CV}(h, b) = \frac{1}{W} \sum_{i=1}^n w_i \int \hat f_{-i}(y \mid x_i)^2\, dy - \frac{2}{W} \sum_{i=1}^n w_i\, \hat f_{-i}(y_i \mid x_i),\]

where $\hat f_{-i}$ leaves observation $i$ out of the numerator and the denominator of the estimate.

Local polynomial quantile regression

The local polynomial estimate of the conditional quantile $q_\tau(x)$ of order $\tau$ (Yu and Jones 1998) minimizes at each point $x_0$ the kernel-weighted check loss,

\[\hat\beta = \arg\min_\beta \sum_{i=1}^n w_i\, k\Big(\frac{x_i - x_0}{h}\Big)\, \rho_\tau\Big(y_i - \sum_{j=0}^p \beta_j (x_i - x_0)^j\Big), \qquad \rho_\tau(u) = u\,(\tau - 1\{u < 0\}),\]

and $\nu!\, \hat\beta_\nu$ estimates the derivative of order $\nu$ of $q_\tau$. Each local fit is a linear program, which the simplex method solves exactly on its dual,

\[\max_a \sum_i v_i y_i a_i \quad \text{subject to} \quad \sum_i v_i z_i a_i = (1 - \tau) \sum_i v_i z_i, \quad 0 \le a_i \le 1,\]

with $v_i$ the kernel weights times the weights of the observations and $z_i$ the local design; the multipliers of the constraints are $\hat\beta$, and the fit interpolates $p + 1$ observations of its window. The default bandwidth, YuJones, rescales the cross-validation bandwidth $h$ of local polynomial mean regression,

\[h_\tau = h \Big(\frac{\tau (1 - \tau)}{\phi(\Phi^{-1}(\tau))^2}\Big)^{1/5}.\]

The conventional standard errors come from the sandwich of a weighted quantile regression, $\tau(1 - \tau)\, s^2 (Z^\top V Z)^{-1} (Z^\top V^2 Z) (Z^\top V Z)^{-1}$, with the sparsity $s = 1/f(q_\tau(x_0) \mid x_0)$ estimated by the difference quotient of local fits at the orders $\tau \pm \delta$, $\delta$ the bandwidth of Hall and Sheather (1988). This replaces the density-weighted bread by the density at $x_0$ times the weighted design Gram matrix, a local approximation rather than an identity.

By default, confint returns robust bias-corrected pointwise intervals. A quantile fit of order $p + 1$ at the same $\tau$ estimates the bias, with pilot bandwidth $b = h / \mathtt{rho}$ by default (rho = 1). A numeric pilot supplies $b$ directly and overrides rho. With $u_i = (x_i - x_0)/h$, $r_p(u) = (1, u, \ldots, u^p)^\top$, $\Gamma_p = \sum_i w_i k(u_i) r_p(u_i) r_p(u_i)^\top$, $g = \Gamma_p^{-1} e_\nu$ and $\Lambda = \sum_i w_i k(u_i) u_i^{p+1} r_p(u_i)$, the estimated bias is

\[\hat B_\nu = \nu!\, h^{p+1-\nu}\, (g^\top\Lambda)\, \hat\beta^{(b)}_{p+1},\]

where $\hat\beta^{(b)}_{p+1}$ is the unscaled pilot coefficient. The robust standard error uses the influence weights of both quantile fits over the union of their windows. It includes the variability of the correction and its covariance with the primary estimate, using the same sparsity as the conventional standard error. This specializes the joint-score argument of Qu and Yoon (2019, Lemma 3 and Remark 2) to an interior point.

Inference assumes independent observations, a conditional density continuous and positive near the quantile, a quantile function smooth of order $p + 2$, identified local designs, and shrinking bandwidths with enough observations in both windows. The correction is computed for every degree and derivative, but validity is claimed only for odd $p - \nu$, including the default degree = derivative + 1. For even differences, higher-order quantile and density terms can lead the bias and need not be removed by this correction.

coef and stderror remain conventional by default; pass method = RobustBiasCorrected() for the corrected estimate and robust standard error. Use confint(q; method = Conventional()) for intervals that require negligible bias. coeftable reports both methods. A failed pilot leaves the conventional results available. Failed sparsity leaves both estimates available, with both standard errors and intervals NaN. The default also applies through rearrange.

Example

using KernelSmoothing

x = randn(500); y = sin.(x) .+ 0.4 .* (1 .+ x .^ 2 ./ 2) .* randn(500)
m = fit(ConditionalDensity, x, y)        # bandwidths by likelihood cross-validation
pdf(m, 0.5, 1.0), cdf(m, 0.5, 1.0), quantile(m, 0.9, 1.0)
conditional(m, 1.0)                      # the conditional distribution at x = 1, a KDE

q = fit(LocalQuantileRegression, x, y; tau = 0.9, points = -2:0.25:2)
coef(q)                                 # conventional estimates
coef(q; method = RobustBiasCorrected()), confint(q)
coeftable(q)                            # both estimates, standard errors and intervals

KernelSmoothing.ConditionalDensity — Type
ConditionalDensity

A kernel estimate of the conditional density of y given x (Rosenblatt 1969; Hyndman, Bashtannyk and Grunwald 1996; Fan, Yao and Tong 1996),

f̂(y | x) = ∑ᵢ ℓᵢ(x) K((y - yᵢ)/b) / b,

where K is the kernel in y with bandwidth b, and ℓᵢ(x) are the weights of the local polynomial fit of order degree at x with the kernel k and bandwidth h in x: the estimate smooths the kernels of the responses as a local polynomial regression smooths the responses. With degree = 0, the weights are those of Nadaraya and Watson, wᵢ k((xᵢ - x)/h) / ∑ⱼ wⱼ k((xⱼ - x)/h), nonnegative and summing to one, so that f̂(⋅ | x) is a density, and conditional(m, x) returns it as a KDE. With degree = 1 (Fan, Yao and Tong 1996), the bias at a boundary of x is smaller, but the weights can be negative, and so can the estimate.

Fitted with fit(ConditionalDensity, x, y); pdf(m, y, x) and cdf(m, y, x) evaluate the estimate and its distribution function, quantile(m, p, x) the conditional quantiles, and bandwidth(m) returns (h, b).

KernelSmoothing.ConditionalCV — Type
ConditionalCV(; criterion = :likelihood, gridsize = 7)

Cross-validation for the bandwidths (h, b) of a ConditionalDensity with degree = 0. With criterion = :likelihood (the default), it maximizes the leave-one-out log-likelihood

∑ᵢ wᵢ log f̂₋ᵢ(yᵢ | xᵢ),

where f̂₋ᵢ leaves observation i out of the numerator and the denominator of the local constant estimate. With criterion = :leastsquares, it minimizes the estimate of the integrated squared error of Hall, Racine and Li (2004),

∑ᵢ wᵢ ∫ f̂₋ᵢ(y | xᵢ)² dy / W - 2 ∑ᵢ wᵢ f̂₋ᵢ(yᵢ | xᵢ) / W,

which takes a time of order n³. Observations with no other observation in their window in x are left out of the criteria, so that an isolated x does not set a floor on h. The search starts on a grid of gridsize × gridsize pairs, from an eighth to four times the normal-reference bandwidths, which give the kernels the standard deviations 1.06 σ̂ n^(-1/6) of each variable, and refines the best pair by the method of Nelder and Mead on the logarithms of the bandwidths, within a factor of 1000 of the reference ones; a warning tells when the search ends near that limit, as when tied responses let the likelihood grow without bound as b shrinks.

KernelSmoothing.LocalQuantileRegression — Type
LocalQuantileRegression

Local polynomial estimates of a conditional quantile of y given x and of its derivatives (Yu and Jones 1998): at a point x₀, the fit of order degree minimizes the kernel-weighted check loss

∑ᵢ wᵢ k((xᵢ - x₀)/h) ρ_τ(yᵢ - β₀ - β₁ (xᵢ - x₀) - ⋯ - β_p (xᵢ - x₀)^p),
ρ_τ(u) = u (τ - 1{u < 0}),

and ν! β̂_ν estimates the derivative of order ν of the quantile function q_τ(x). Each local fit is a linear program, solved exactly by the simplex method, so that the fit interpolates degree + 1 observations of its window. Fitted with fit(LocalQuantileRegression, x, y); coef(m) and stderror(m) describe the conventional estimates. With method = RobustBiasCorrected() they describe the bias-corrected estimates, using a quantile fit of order p + 1 with pilot bandwidth b. The default intervals of confint are robust bias-corrected, with the variability of both fits and their covariance included.

Pointwise inference assumes independent observations, a conditional density f(y | x) continuous and positive near q_τ(x₀), a quantile function smooth of order p + 2, identified local designs, and h, b → 0 with enough observations in both windows. Replacing the density-weighted bread by f(q_τ(x₀) | x₀) times the weighted design Gram matrix is a local approximation, not an identity. The joint quantile-score argument specializes Qu and Yoon (2019, Lemma 3 and Remark 2) to an interior point. Bias correction is computed for every degree and derivative, but validity is claimed only for odd degree - derivative, as in the default degree = derivative + 1. For even differences, higher-order quantile and density terms can lead the bias and need not be removed by this correction.

StatsAPI.fit — Method
fit(LocalQuantileRegression, x, y; tau = 0.5, bandwidth = YuJones(), derivative = 0,
    degree = derivative + 1, kernel = EpanechnikovKernel(), pilot = nothing, rho = 1,
    points = nothing, weights = nothing)

Local polynomial estimates of the quantile of order tau of y given x, or of its derivative of order derivative, at points (by default 30 points between the quantiles of order 0.025 and 0.975 of x). The bandwidth is a positive number or a rule, by default YuJones(), which rescales a bandwidth for the mean regression of the same degree. The kernel must be nonnegative. quantreg's lprq estimates the derivative from the same local linear fits, degree = 1.

The pilot fit of order degree + 1 at the same quantile estimates the bias. Its bandwidth is b = h / rho by default, so rho = 1 uses the same bandwidth for both fits. A numeric pilot overrides rho, which is then not validated; b must be positive and finite after conversion to Float64. Each fit uses one bandwidth for all points. The pilot is fitted immediately, while the sparsity for standard errors is estimated only when requested. A failed pilot leaves the conventional results available. The assumptions and the odd degree - derivative validity scope are given under LocalQuantileRegression.

StatsAPI.coef — Method
coef(m::LocalQuantileRegression; method = Conventional())

The quantile or derivative estimates at the points. With method = RobustBiasCorrected(), subtract the bias estimated by the quantile fit of order degree + 1 with bandwidth b. These are the centres of the default intervals of confint; they are NaN where the primary fit or pilot fails. Only Conventional() and RobustBiasCorrected() are supported. See LocalQuantileRegression for the assumptions and the odd degree - derivative validity scope.

StatsAPI.stderror — Method
stderror(m::LocalQuantileRegression; method = Conventional())

The standard errors of the estimates, from the sandwich of a weighted quantile regression (Koenker 2005, Section 3.4) with the conditional density at the quantile estimated locally: at a point, the variance of β̂ is about

τ (1 - τ) s² (ZᵀWZ)⁻¹ (ZᵀW²Z) (ZᵀWZ)⁻¹,

where W holds the kernel weights times the weights of the observations and s is the sparsity 1 / f(q_τ(x₀) | x₀), estimated by the difference quotient (q̂_{τ+δ}(x₀) - q̂_{τ-δ}(x₀)) / (2δ) of local fits at the orders τ ± δ, with the bandwidth δ of Hall and Sheather (1988) for the effective number of observations in the window. Where the two fits do not increase, as for discrete responses, the sparsity cannot be estimated and both standard errors are NaN; the estimates remain available.

With method = RobustBiasCorrected(), use the influence weights of both quantile fits over the union of their windows, including their covariance and the variability of the bias correction. Both standard errors use the same sparsity, estimated lazily from the order-degree fits with bandwidth h. A failed pilot leaves only the robust standard error missing. Only Conventional() and RobustBiasCorrected() are supported. The conditional density approximation and the odd degree - derivative validity scope are stated under LocalQuantileRegression. Conventional intervals require negligible bias.

StatsAPI.confint — Method
confint(m::LocalQuantileRegression; level = 0.95, method = RobustBiasCorrected())

Pointwise intervals coef(m; method) ± z stderror(m; method) at the points, as a matrix with the lower and upper ends as columns. By default these are robust bias-corrected intervals. With method = Conventional(), they are centred at the conventional estimates and require negligible bias. Only these two methods are supported. See LocalQuantileRegression for the assumptions, including the odd degree - derivative validity scope of the correction. Intervals obtained through rearrange inherit this default.

StatsAPI.coeftable — Method
coeftable(m::LocalQuantileRegression; level = 0.95)

The results as a named tuple of equal-length columns: point, bandwidths h and b, the conventional estimate, standard error se and interval lower, upper, and the bias-corrected estimate_bc, robust standard error se_rbc and interval lower_rbc, upper_rbc. Sparsity is estimated once per point for both standard errors. A failed pilot affects only the corrected results; failed sparsity affects both standard errors and intervals, leaving the estimates available. The assumptions and the odd degree - derivative validity scope are given under LocalQuantileRegression.

KernelSmoothing.YuJones — Type
YuJones(; rule = CrossValidation())

The bandwidth of Yu and Jones (1998) for local polynomial quantile regression,

h_τ = h (τ (1 - τ) / φ(Φ⁻¹(τ))²)^(1/5),

which rescales the bandwidth h that rule chooses for local polynomial mean regression of the same degree by the ratio of the asymptotically optimal bandwidths for a conditional quantile and a conditional mean when the errors are normal and homoscedastic. It widens the bandwidth away from the median, where fewer observations inform a quantile.
