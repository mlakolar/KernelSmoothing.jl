Conditional densities and quantiles

Conditional density estimation

For observations $(x_i, y_i)$ with weights $w_i$, the kernel estimate of the conditional density of $y$ given $x$ smooths the kernels of the responses as a local polynomial regression smooths the responses:

\[\hat f(y \mid x) = \sum_{i=1}^n \ell_i(x)\, \frac{1}{b} K\Big(\frac{y - y_i}{b}\Big),\]

where $K$ is the kernel in $y$ with bandwidth $b$ and $\ell_i(x)$ are the weights of the local polynomial fit of order $p$ at $x$ with kernel $k$ and bandwidth $h$. For $p = 0$ (Rosenblatt 1969; Hyndman, Bashtannyk and Grunwald 1996),

\[\ell_i(x) = \frac{w_i\, k((x_i - x)/h)}{\sum_j w_j\, k((x_j - x)/h)},\]

which are nonnegative and sum to one, so that $\hat f(\cdot \mid x)$ is a density: conditional(m, x) returns it as a KDE of the responses with these weights, with its distribution function and quantiles. For $p = 1$ (Fan, Yao and Tong 1996), the estimate has a smaller bias near the ends of the range of $x$, but the weights can be negative.

ConditionalCV chooses $(h, b)$ for $p = 0$ by maximizing the leave-one-out log-likelihood $\sum_i w_i \log \hat f_{-i}(y_i \mid x_i)$, the default of the R package np, or by minimizing the least-squares criterion of Hall, Racine and Li (2004),

\[\mathrm{CV}(h, b) = \frac{1}{W} \sum_{i=1}^n w_i \int \hat f_{-i}(y \mid x_i)^2\, dy - \frac{2}{W} \sum_{i=1}^n w_i\, \hat f_{-i}(y_i \mid x_i),\]

where $\hat f_{-i}$ leaves observation $i$ out of the numerator and the denominator of the estimate.

Local polynomial quantile regression

The local polynomial estimate of the conditional quantile $q_\tau(x)$ of order $\tau$ (Yu and Jones 1998) minimizes at each point $x_0$ the kernel-weighted check loss,

\[\hat\beta = \arg\min_\beta \sum_{i=1}^n w_i\, k\Big(\frac{x_i - x_0}{h}\Big)\, \rho_\tau\Big(y_i - \sum_{j=0}^p \beta_j (x_i - x_0)^j\Big), \qquad \rho_\tau(u) = u\,(\tau - 1\{u < 0\}),\]

and $\nu!\, \hat\beta_\nu$ estimates the derivative of order $\nu$ of $q_\tau$. Each local fit is a linear program, which the simplex method solves exactly on its dual,

\[\max_a \sum_i v_i y_i a_i \quad \text{subject to} \quad \sum_i v_i z_i a_i = (1 - \tau) \sum_i v_i z_i, \quad 0 \le a_i \le 1,\]

with $v_i$ the kernel weights times the weights of the observations and $z_i$ the local design; the multipliers of the constraints are $\hat\beta$, and the fit interpolates $p + 1$ observations of its window. The default bandwidth, YuJones, rescales the cross-validation bandwidth $h$ of local polynomial mean regression,

\[h_\tau = h \Big(\frac{\tau (1 - \tau)}{\phi(\Phi^{-1}(\tau))^2}\Big)^{1/5}.\]

The standard errors come from the sandwich of a weighted quantile regression, $\tau(1 - \tau)\, s^2 (Z^\top V Z)^{-1} (Z^\top V^2 Z) (Z^\top V Z)^{-1}$, with the sparsity $s = 1/f(q_\tau(x_0) \mid x_0)$ estimated by the difference quotient of local fits at the orders $\tau \pm \delta$, $\delta$ the bandwidth of Hall and Sheather (1988). They ignore the bias.

Example

using KernelSmoothing

x = randn(500); y = sin.(x) .+ 0.4 .* (1 .+ x .^ 2 ./ 2) .* randn(500)
m = fit(ConditionalDensity, x, y)        # bandwidths by likelihood cross-validation
pdf(m, 0.5, 1.0), cdf(m, 0.5, 1.0), quantile(m, 0.9, 1.0)
conditional(m, 1.0)                      # the conditional distribution at x = 1, a KDE

q = fit(LocalQuantileRegression, x, y; tau = 0.9, points = -2:0.25:2)
coef(q), confint(q)

Reference

KernelSmoothing.ConditionalDensity — Type
ConditionalDensity

A kernel estimate of the conditional density of y given x (Rosenblatt 1969; Hyndman, Bashtannyk and Grunwald 1996; Fan, Yao and Tong 1996),

f̂(y | x) = ∑ᵢ ℓᵢ(x) K((y - yᵢ)/b) / b,

where K is the kernel in y with bandwidth b, and ℓᵢ(x) are the weights of the local polynomial fit of order degree at x with the kernel k and bandwidth h in x: the estimate smooths the kernels of the responses as a local polynomial regression smooths the responses. With degree = 0, the weights are those of Nadaraya and Watson, wᵢ k((xᵢ - x)/h) / ∑ⱼ wⱼ k((xⱼ - x)/h), nonnegative and summing to one, so that f̂(⋅ | x) is a density, and conditional(m, x) returns it as a KDE. With degree = 1 (Fan, Yao and Tong 1996), the bias at a boundary of x is smaller, but the weights can be negative, and so can the estimate.

Fitted with fit(ConditionalDensity, x, y); pdf(m, y, x) and cdf(m, y, x) evaluate the estimate and its distribution function, quantile(m, p, x) the conditional quantiles, and bandwidth(m) returns (h, b).

KernelSmoothing.ConditionalCV — Type
ConditionalCV(; criterion = :likelihood, gridsize = 7)

Cross-validation for the bandwidths (h, b) of a ConditionalDensity with degree = 0. With criterion = :likelihood (the default), it maximizes the leave-one-out log-likelihood

∑ᵢ wᵢ log f̂₋ᵢ(yᵢ | xᵢ),

where f̂₋ᵢ leaves observation i out of the numerator and the denominator of the local constant estimate. With criterion = :leastsquares, it minimizes the estimate of the integrated squared error of Hall, Racine and Li (2004),

∑ᵢ wᵢ ∫ f̂₋ᵢ(y | xᵢ)² dy / W - 2 ∑ᵢ wᵢ f̂₋ᵢ(yᵢ | xᵢ) / W,

which takes a time of order n³. Observations with no other observation in their window in x are left out of the criteria, so that an isolated x does not set a floor on h. The search starts on a grid of gridsize × gridsize pairs, from an eighth to four times the normal-reference bandwidths, which give the kernels the standard deviations 1.06 σ̂ n^(-1/6) of each variable, and refines the best pair by the method of Nelder and Mead on the logarithms of the bandwidths, within a factor of 1000 of the reference ones; a warning tells when the search ends near that limit, as when tied responses let the likelihood grow without bound as b shrinks.

KernelSmoothing.LocalQuantileRegression — Type
LocalQuantileRegression

Local polynomial estimates of a conditional quantile of y given x and of its derivatives (Yu and Jones 1998): at a point x₀, the fit of order degree minimizes the kernel-weighted check loss

∑ᵢ wᵢ k((xᵢ - x₀)/h) ρ_τ(yᵢ - β₀ - β₁ (xᵢ - x₀) - ⋯ - β_p (xᵢ - x₀)^p),
ρ_τ(u) = u (τ - 1{u < 0}),

and ν! β̂_ν estimates the derivative of order ν of the quantile function q_τ(x). Each local fit is a linear program, solved exactly by the simplex method, so that the fit interpolates degree + 1 observations of its window. Fitted with fit(LocalQuantileRegression, x, y); coef(m) returns the estimates at the points, stderror(m) and confint(m; level) the standard errors and intervals of stderror.

source
StatsAPI.fit — Method
fit(LocalQuantileRegression, x, y; tau = 0.5, bandwidth = YuJones(), derivative = 0,
    degree = derivative + 1, kernel = EpanechnikovKernel(), points = nothing, weights = nothing)

Local polynomial estimates of the quantile of order tau of y given x, or of its derivative of order derivative, at points (by default 30 points between the quantiles of order 0.025 and 0.975 of x). The bandwidth is a positive number or a rule, by default YuJones(), which rescales a bandwidth for the mean regression of the same degree. The kernel must be nonnegative. quantreg's lprq estimates the derivative from the same local linear fits, degree = 1.

source
StatsAPI.stderror — Method
stderror(m::LocalQuantileRegression)

The standard errors of the estimates, from the sandwich of a weighted quantile regression (Koenker 2005, Section 3.4) with the conditional density at the quantile estimated locally: at a point, the variance of β̂ is about

τ (1 - τ) s² (ZᵀWZ)⁻¹ (ZᵀW²Z) (ZᵀWZ)⁻¹,

where W holds the kernel weights times the weights of the observations and s is the sparsity 1 / f(q_τ(x₀) | x₀), estimated by the difference quotient (q̂_{τ+δ}(x₀) - q̂_{τ-δ}(x₀)) / (2δ) of local fits at the orders τ ± δ, with the bandwidth δ of Hall and Sheather (1988) for the effective number of observations in the window. Where the two fits do not increase, as for discrete responses, the sparsity cannot be estimated and the standard error is NaN. They describe the variability of the estimates, not their bias, so that intervals from them cover only when the bandwidth is small enough for the bias to be negligible.

source
StatsAPI.confint — Method
confint(m::LocalQuantileRegression; level = 0.95)

The intervals coef(m) ± z stderror(m) at the points, as a matrix with the lower and upper ends as columns; like the standard errors, they ignore the bias.

source
KernelSmoothing.YuJones — Type
YuJones(; rule = CrossValidation())

The bandwidth of Yu and Jones (1998) for local polynomial quantile regression,

h_τ = h (τ (1 - τ) / φ(Φ⁻¹(τ))²)^(1/5),

which rescales the bandwidth h that rule chooses for local polynomial mean regression of the same degree by the ratio of the asymptotically optimal bandwidths for a conditional quantile and a conditional mean when the errors are normal and homoscedastic. It widens the bandwidth away from the median, where fewer observations inform a quantile.
