KernelSmoothing.jl

Kernel density estimation and local polynomial regression, with data-driven bandwidth selection and robust bias-corrected inference. The package is under development.

It provides:

  • kernel density estimates that are distributions of Distributions.jl, robust bias-corrected intervals for densities at interior points, and the local polynomial density estimator of Cattaneo, Jansson and Ma for densities with a boundary;
  • multivariate kernel density estimates with a bandwidth matrix, chosen by normal scale, plug-in, smoothed or least-squares cross-validation rules;
  • density estimates on a bounded support that are distributions there: reflection, beta and gamma kernels, and transformations;
  • local polynomial regression of a regression function or its derivatives, with conventional, robust bias-corrected and honest intervals, covariances between points and uniform bands;
  • conditional densities and local polynomial quantile regression;
  • shape constraints: rearranged estimates and bands, and projections onto monotone and convex sequences;
  • regression discontinuity estimates for sharp, fuzzy and kink designs, with covariates, and the manipulation test of the density at the cutoff; designs with several cutoffs, power calculations and randomization inference under local randomization;
  • bandwidth selectors: rules of thumb, Sheather and Jones, cross-validation and plug-in bandwidths.

The estimators are built in layers. Kernels are the smoothing kernels with their distribution functions, moments, derivatives, convolutions and products. Prepared data is a sample sorted once, with its weights and ties recorded. Kernel sums are the sums $\sum_i w_i z_i K(u_i) u_i^r$ that all estimators are built from, computed by exact direct, windowed and fast engines or approximately from binned data.

KernelSmoothing

Kernel density estimation and local polynomial regression, with data-driven bandwidth selection and robust bias-corrected inference.

The estimators are KDE, a kernel density estimate that is a distribution of Distributions.jl; PointwiseDensity, density estimates at a set of points with robust bias-corrected intervals; LocalPolynomialDensity, the boundary-adaptive density estimator of Cattaneo, Jansson and Ma; LocalPolynomialRegression, local polynomial estimates of a regression function or its derivatives with conventional, robust bias-corrected and Honest intervals and uniform bands, see conf_band; RegressionDiscontinuity, estimates for regression discontinuity designs; MultivariateKDE, a kernel density estimate in several dimensions with a bandwidth matrix; BoundedKDE, a density estimate on a bounded support; ConditionalDensity, a kernel estimate of a conditional density; and LocalQuantileRegression, local polynomial quantile regression. They are fitted with fit and choose their bandwidths with a BandwidthSelector. Underneath are the smoothing kernels, see AbstractKernel, samples prepared for smoothing, see SmoothingData, and the kernel sums that many of the estimators and bandwidth selectors are built from, see kernel_sums.

Example

using KernelSmoothing

x = randn(1000)
f = fit(KDE, x)                      # Epanechnikov kernel, Sheather–Jones bandwidth
pdf(f, 0.0), cdf(f, 0.0), quantile(f, 0.975)
pdf.(f, range(-3, 3; length = 401))  # many points at once, exactly
derivative(f, 1)(0.5)                # the estimated derivative of the density
confint(fit(PointwiseDensity, x; points = [-1, 0, 1]))   # robust bias-corrected intervals

t = rand(2000); y = sin.(2π .* t) .+ 0.3 .* randn(2000)
m = fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, t, y; points = range(0, 1; length = 21))
coef(m), confint(m)                  # estimates and robust bias-corrected intervals
conf_band(m)                          # a uniform band over the 21 points

Defaults and agreement with R

The estimators follow R's nprobust, lpdensity and RDHonest closely. With given bandwidths, the estimates and standard errors agree with these packages to rounding, as the tests check against reference values from them. The defaults are the following.

estimatorkernelbandwidthcompat = true gives
fit(KDE, x)EpanechnikovSheatherJonesbandwidth = SheatherJones(compat = true): R's bw.SJ
fit(PointwiseDensity, x)Epanechnikovplug-in, pilots anchored on Sheather–Jonesnprobust's kdrobust
fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y)Epanechnikov, degree derivative + 1plug-in, pilots anchored on cross-validationnprobust's lprobust
fit(LocalPolynomialDensity, x)triangular, degree 2lpdensity's plug-in, with a bound on the biaslpdensity

The page Comparison with R lists what the tests verify against each R package, explains every place where the defaults depart from it, and translates the argument names.

The pages of the estimators give the details.

For all fits, coef(m) and stderror(m) describe the conventional estimates, and coef(m; method = RobustBiasCorrected()) and stderror(m; method = RobustBiasCorrected()) give the bias-corrected estimates and their robust standard errors. The intervals of confint(m) and the bands of conf_band(m) are robust bias-corrected by default and centred at the bias-corrected estimates, since conventional intervals cover only for bandwidths well below the ones that minimize the mean squared error. coeftable(m) lists both.

Bandwidth convention

The compactly supported kernels live on [-1, 1], so the bandwidth h is the half-width of the smoothing window: an observation gets weight zero unless it is within h of the point. The Gaussian kernel is the standard normal density, so its bandwidth is a standard deviation. KernelDensity.jl, KDEpy and R's density instead scale every kernel to unit variance; their bandwidth h_sd corresponds to the half-width h_sd / sqrt(moment(k, 2)) here, for example h_sd √5 for the Epanechnikov kernel.

Plotting

Loading Plots.jl, or another package that uses RecipesBase, adds plot recipes. plot(m) draws the estimates of a pointwise fit (LocalPolynomialRegression, PointwiseDensity or LocalPolynomialDensity) over its shaded robust bias-corrected intervals, and plot(f) the density estimate of a KDE. The keywords level and method choose the intervals as for confint, uniform = true draws the uniform band of conf_band instead, and data = true adds the observations of a regression.

using KernelSmoothing, Plots
m = fit(LocalPolynomialRegression, x, y)
plot(m; data = true)
plot(m; uniform = true, level = 0.9)

Threads

Kernel sums, fits at several points and cross-validation run in parallel when Julia is started with several threads, for example with julia --threads=auto. The results do not depend on the number of threads.

Installation

Requires Julia 1.10 or later. The package is not registered; install it from GitHub:

using Pkg
Pkg.add(url = "https://github.com/mlakolar/KernelSmoothing.jl")

Development tests

From a master checkout's package root, run:

julia scripts/test_master.jl

This prepares the dedicated master-based test environment, including test extras and sibling workspace dependencies, and runs the complete suite. Package version numbers and dependency declarations remain unchanged.

Acknowledgements

KernelSmoothing.jl is an independent implementation. We wrote its procedures from the papers that proposed them, and we validated them against the R packages below, whose authors made these methods available to applied researchers. With compat = true, the results agree with these packages on the configurations in the test suite; the defaults depart from them where the documentation says so. If you use the package, please cite the papers of the methods you use.
