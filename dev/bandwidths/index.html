Bandwidth selection

Estimators accept a number or a BandwidthSelector as bandwidth. selectbandwidth applies a rule and returns a BandwidthSelection that records what the rule computed, such as pilot bandwidths or a cross-validation curve.

For densities, SheatherJones is the default; LSCV, NormalReference and SilvermanRule are alternatives. For regression, the default follows nprobust: MSEOptimal at a single point and IMSEOptimal at several, the plug-in bandwidths of Calonico, Cattaneo and Farrell. CoverageOptimal rescales them for robust bias-corrected inference, and RuleOfThumb and CrossValidation are alternatives.

d = SmoothingData(randn(1000))
s = selectbandwidth(LSCV(), d, EpanechnikovKernel())
s.h, s.details.minimizers        # the bandwidth, and all local minimizers of the criterion
KernelSmoothing.BandwidthSelection — Type
BandwidthSelection

The result of selectbandwidth: the bandwidth h, a number or a vector with one bandwidth per evaluation point, the rule that chose it (nothing for a bandwidth given by the user) and the named tuple details with what the rule computed on the way, such as the scale estimate of a rule of thumb.

KernelSmoothing.BandwidthSelector — Type
BandwidthSelector

Supertype of the rules that choose a bandwidth from the data, such as NormalReference and SilvermanRule. selectbandwidth applies a rule and records what it computed on the way, and bandwidth returns only the bandwidth. Estimators accept a rule wherever they accept a bandwidth.

A new rule R <: BandwidthSelector implements selectbandwidth(rule::R, d::SmoothingData, k::AbstractKernel).

KernelSmoothing.NormalReference — Type
NormalReference(; robust = true)

The bandwidth that minimizes the asymptotic mean integrated squared error when the density is normal,

h = σ̂ (8 √π R(k) / (3 μ₂(k)² n))^(1/5),

where R(k) = roughness(k), μ₂(k) = moment(k, 2) and n is the effective_nobs. The scale σ̂ is the standard deviation or, with robust = true, the smaller of the standard deviation and the interquartile range divided by 1.349, which guards against heavy tails and outliers. For the Gaussian kernel the rule gives 1.06 σ̂ n^(-1/5) and for the Epanechnikov kernel 2.34 σ̂ n^(-1/5).

The rule oversmooths densities that are far from normal, multimodal ones in particular. It is a quick default and a pilot for better selectors. It needs a second-order kernel, with moment(k, 2) > 0.

source
KernelSmoothing.SilvermanRule — Type
SilvermanRule()

Silverman's rule of thumb, 0.9 min(σ̂, IQR / 1.34) n^(-1/5) for the Gaussian kernel, where σ̂ is the standard deviation, IQR the interquartile range and n the effective_nobs. It is the default bandwidth of R's density (bw.nrd0) and of KernelDensity.jl. Other kernels get the bandwidth that R's density uses for them, the Gaussian one divided by the standard deviation of the kernel, sqrt(moment(k, 2)). Compared with NormalReference, the factor 0.9 instead of 1.06 suits densities with several modes better.

KernelSmoothing.selectbandwidth — Function
selectbandwidth(rule::BandwidthSelector, d::SmoothingData, k::AbstractKernel)

Applies the bandwidth selector rule to the sample d for the kernel k and returns a BandwidthSelection. Bandwidths follow the convention of AbstractKernel: a half-width for the compactly supported kernels and a standard deviation for the Gaussian kernel.

KernelSmoothing.CrossValidation — Type
CrossValidation(; gridsize = 40)

Leave-one-out cross-validation for local polynomial regression: the bandwidth that minimizes

CV(h) = ∑ᵢ wᵢ (yᵢ - m̂₋ᵢ(xᵢ))² / ∑ᵢ wᵢ,   m̂₋ᵢ(xᵢ) = (m̂(xᵢ) - ℓᵢᵢ yᵢ) / (1 - ℓᵢᵢ),

where m̂₋ᵢ(xᵢ) is the fit at xᵢ without observation i and ℓᵢᵢ the weight of yᵢ in the fit at xᵢ. The fits at all observations come from kernelsums, so that a bandwidth costs O(n) operations for polynomial kernels. The grid has gridsize bandwidths spaced evenly on the log scale, from the smallest one that leaves p + 1 other distinct values in every window, below which leave-one-out fits break down, to the range of the data. The global minimizer on the grid is refined, and the details record the grid and the criterion. Cross-validation estimates the regression function, so it needs derivative = 0.

source
KernelSmoothing.LSCV — Type
LSCV(; gridsize = 60, exact = 1000)

Least-squares cross-validation for kernel density estimates (Rudemo 1982; Bowman 1984). It minimizes an unbiased estimate of the integrated squared error minus ∫ f²,

CV(h) = ∫ f̂(x)² dx - 2 ∑_{i ≠ j} wᵢ wⱼ k((xᵢ - xⱼ) / h) / (h (W² - ∑ᵢ wᵢ²)),

over gridsize bandwidths spaced evenly on the log scale between h_OS / 100 and 4 h_OS, where h_OS is the oversmoothed bandwidth of Terrell (1990), an upper bound for the bandwidth that minimizes the asymptotic mean integrated squared error. The minimizer of the criterion itself can exceed h_OS, as it does for normal data about half of the time, hence the factor 4. Following Sheather (2004), it takes the largest local minimizer on the grid and refines it. The details of the selection record the grid, the criterion, all local minimizers and whether the minimum was at an end of the grid, which happens, for example, when ties make the criterion fall without bound as h shrinks.

Polynomial kernels use exact fast sums. Gaussian kernels use exact double sums for up to exact observations and binned ones otherwise, as in SheatherJones; since the criterion is flat near its minimum, binning moves the bandwidth by about 1e-4 relative. Cross-validation converges slowly, at the relative rate n^(-1/10), and varies a lot between samples, so it is an alternative to SheatherJones rather than a default.

KernelSmoothing.SheatherJones — Type
SheatherJones(; method = :ste, exact = 100)

The plug-in bandwidth of Sheather and Jones (1991) for kernel density estimates. It estimates ψ₄ = ∫ f''(x)² dx with a Gaussian pilot kernel and plugs the estimate into the bandwidth that minimizes the asymptotic mean integrated squared error,

h = (R(k) / (μ₂(k)² ψ̂₄(g) n))^(1/5),

where R(k) = roughness(k), μ₂(k) = moment(k, 2) and n is the effective_nobs. With method = :ste, "solve the equation", the pilot bandwidth g = α₂ h^(5/7) depends on h itself, with α₂ estimated from ψ̂₄ and ψ̂₆ at normal-reference pilot bandwidths, and the equation is solved to a relative accuracy of 1e-10. With method = :dpi, the direct plug-in, g is the bandwidth that is optimal for estimating ψ₄ when ψ₆ is estimated at its normal-reference pilot bandwidth.

The estimates are the double sums of Sheather and Jones,

ψ̂ᵣ(g) = ∑ᵢ ∑ⱼ wᵢ wⱼ φ_g⁽ʳ⁾(xᵢ - xⱼ) / (W² - ∑ᵢ wᵢ²),   W = ∑ᵢ wᵢ,

with the diagonal included, which with equal weights is the definition of R's bw.SJ. They are computed exactly for up to exact observations and otherwise from the data binned linearly once, on a grid with about 100 points per smallest pilot bandwidth, with the lagged products of the bin weights from an FFT. This changes the estimates by about 1e-5 and the bandwidth by about 1e-6 relative, far below its statistical error, and makes the selection take a few milliseconds for up to 10^6 observations. R's bw.SJ bins differences of pairs of observations coarsely and stops its search early, so by default it agrees with this rule to two or three digits only. With fine bins it agrees to about 1e-6 for method = :dpi and 4e-5 for :ste, where R rounds the constant (2 φ⁽⁴⁾(0) μ₂(k)² / R(k))^(1/7) = 1.35733 of the Gaussian kernel to 1.357. This rule is the recommended default for densities without boundaries (Jones, Marron and Sheather 1996).

KernelSmoothing.CoverageOptimal — Type
CoverageOptimal(base = MSEOptimal(); convention = :nprobust)

Bandwidths that make the coverage error of robust bias-corrected intervals shrink at the fastest rate, obtained by rescaling the plug-in bandwidths of base (MSEOptimal or IMSEOptimal) by a power of the sample size (Calonico, Cattaneo and Farrell 2018, 2022).

With convention = :nprobust, the rescaling targets the rate n^(-1/(p+3)), which is coverage-error optimal at boundary points and for even p - ν, at every point: h n^(-p/((2p+3)(p+3))) for odd p - ν and h n^(-(p+2)/((2p+5)(p+3))) for even p - ν, as nprobust's ce-rot. With convention = :interior, an odd p - ν targets the interior rate n^(-1/(p+4)) instead, h n^(-(p-1)/((2p+3)(p+4))), which leaves local linear bandwidths unchanged. The pilot bandwidths are rescaled as in nprobust in both conventions.

KernelSmoothing.IMSEOptimal — Type
IMSEOptimal(; grid = 30, regularization = 1, interior = false)

The direct plug-in estimate of the bandwidth that minimizes the integrated mean squared error of a local polynomial fit, one bandwidth for all points: the variance and squared bias constants of MSEOptimal are averaged over grid equally spaced points between the smallest and the largest observation, as nprobust's imse-dpi does, or with grid = :points over the evaluation points of the fit. The pilot bandwidths are those of MSEOptimal, adaptive by default and nprobust's with compat = true, and b is in details.b.

For a sample without responses, the rule gives the integrated plug-in bandwidth for densities, with the constants of MSEOptimal averaged over the quantiles of order 0.1 to 0.9 (or the evaluation points with grid = :points), or with compat = true nprobust's kdbwselect. For LocalPolynomialDensity, it is lpdensity's imse-dpi, which sums the terms of MSEOptimal over the evaluation points with grid = :points, as lpdensity does, or over grid equally spaced points between the smallest and the largest observation.

KernelSmoothing.MSEOptimal — Type
MSEOptimal(; regularization = 1, interior = false)

The direct plug-in estimate of the bandwidth that minimizes the mean squared error of a local polynomial fit at each evaluation point (Calonico, Cattaneo and Farrell 2019). At each point, the derivative of order p + 1 that drives the bias of the fit of order p is estimated by a fit of order q = p + 1 with the pilot bandwidth b, and h minimizes the estimated mean squared error, with the finite-sample bias and variance constants of the local fits, a preliminary normal-reference bandwidth for the variances, a cap at the distance to the farther end of the data and a floor at the distance of the minobs-th nearest observation. The squared bias carries the regularization term 3 B₁² V̂[β̂] times regularization, which keeps the bandwidth finite where the derivative is nearly zero (Imbens and Kalyanaraman 2012). When p - ν is even and interior = false, the bias includes the next term of its expansion and the mean squared error is minimized numerically.

By default, the pilot bandwidth b is the CrossValidation bandwidth of the fit of order p times the ratio of the RuleOfThumb bandwidths for the derivative of order p + 1 (fit of order q) and for the regression function (fit of order p), so that it adapts to the roughness of the regression function; the pilot for the derivative of order p + 2, which even p - ν needs, is formed in the same way. Cross-validation assumes independent errors: with positively correlated errors it undersmooths, and so does the pilot. With compat = true, the rule is nprobust's lpbwselect with bwselect = "mse-dpi" instead, which agrees with nprobust 1.0.0 to rounding. Its pilot bandwidths come from three more plug-in stages whose bias constants are estimated by fits over the whole range of the data, and at interior points they end at the cap, so that the derivative is estimated over nearly the whole range. For local linear fits, its bandwidths are then about twice the optimal ones at a sharp peak and five times for sin(6πx), where the adaptive pilots come within 10 percent at interior points.

The selection holds one bandwidth per point, and the pilot bandwidths in details.b; fit with pilot = :selected to use them, since by default the fit uses b = h / rho, as nprobust does. With the adaptive pilots, details also holds the cross-validation bandwidth.

For a sample without responses, the rule gives the plug-in bandwidth for the density at each point, h = (R(k) f̂(x) / (4 n (B̂² + regularization R̂)))^(1/5) with B̂ = μ₂(k)/2 f̂''(x) and R̂ = 3 (μ₂(k)/2)² V̂[f̂''(x)]. The pilot f̂'' uses the bandwidth b, the normal-reference ratio of the bandwidths for f'' and for f applied to the SheatherJones bandwidth, so that it adapts to multimodal densities, and f̂ uses the Sheather–Jones bandwidth. With compat = true, it gives nprobust's kdbwselect instead, whose rule-of-thumb pilots assume a normal density, for the Epanechnikov and uniform kernels; for a bimodal density they can make the bandwidth in the valley ten times too large.

For LocalPolynomialDensity, the rule is the plug-in bandwidth of R's lpdensity (bwselect = "mse-dpi"), which minimizes the estimated asymptotic mean squared error a^(2p+2-2v) (B₁ + a B₂)² + σ²/a^(2v-1) at each point, where v = derivative + 1 is the order of the derivative of the distribution function. The derivatives of orders p + 1 and p + 2 in B₁ and B₂ come from local fits of orders p + 2 and p + 3 at rule-of-thumb bandwidths for the whole set of points, and the constants of the bias and the variance from the data at a third such bandwidth. The options regularization and interior do not apply there. Unless compat = true, the squared bias is bounded by (|B₁| + a |B₂|)², since lpdensity's (B₁ + a B₂)² vanishes at a = -B₁/B₂ when the estimated terms have opposite signs.
