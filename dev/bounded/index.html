Densities on a bounded support

A kernel density estimate KDE puts mass outside the support of the data: near a boundary at $a$, the kernels of the observations within a bandwidth of $a$ spill over it, so that the estimate is too low at the boundary and does not integrate to one on the support. fit(BoundedKDE, x; support = (a, b), method) returns an estimate that is a distribution on the support, with pdf, logpdf, cdf, ccdf, quantile, rand, mean and var. Either boundary can be infinite, as in support = (0, Inf). For estimates of the density at a boundary with confidence intervals, see LocalPolynomialDensity.

Methods

Reflection (Schuster 1985). The estimate of the observations and their mirror images at the boundaries (for an upper boundary $b$ alone, the images $2b - x_i$),

\[\hat f(x) = \frac{1}{hW} \sum_{i=1}^n w_i \sum_{m \in \mathbb{Z}} \Big[ K\Big(\frac{x - x_i - 2mL}{h}\Big) + K\Big(\frac{x + x_i - 2a - 2mL}{h}\Big) \Big], \qquad a \leq x \leq b,\]

with $L = b - a$, and only the terms $m = 0$ of the first sum and of the images at $a$ when $b = \infty$. It is the density of $x_i + hε$ folded into $[a, b]$, so that it integrates to one, and it is computed exactly from the observations, with its distribution function. Its derivative at a boundary is zero, so that it has a bias of order $h$ there unless $f'(a) = 0$. The bandwidth rule is applied to the observations and their first images: their estimate is the reflected density, whose optimal bandwidth is that of $f$ on the support.

Beta kernels (Chen 1999), on $[a, b]$, in the variable $u = (x - a)/(b - a)$:

\[\tilde f(u) = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{w_i}{W}\, \beta\Big(u_i; \frac{u}{s} + 1, \frac{1 - u}{s} + 1\Big),\]

where $\beta(\cdot; p, q)$ is the Beta(p, q) density and $s$ the smoothing parameter. The kernel changes its shape with $u$ so that it never leaves the support, and the bias is of order $s$ everywhere, $s\{(1 - 2u) f'(u) + \tfrac12 u(1 - u) f''(u)\}$. The modified estimator of Chen (1999) uses $\beta(\cdot; u/s, (1-u)/s)$ in the interior, whose bias does not involve $f'$, and modified first and second parameters within $2s$ of the boundaries.

Gamma kernels (Chen 2000), on $[a, \infty)$, with $u = x - a$: $\tilde f(u) = \sum_i (w_i/W)\, \gamma(u_i; u/s + 1, s)$, the gamma density with shape $u/s + 1$ and scale $s$, and the modified version with shape $u/s$ above $2s$ and $(u/(2s))^2 + 1$ below.

Neither the beta nor the gamma kernel estimate integrates to one. The estimate is $\tilde f$ divided by its integral, computed by Gauss–Legendre quadrature on panels a quarter of the spread of the kernel wide, over the range where the estimate is not negligible; the same panels give the distribution function and its upper tail, and the quantiles invert it within a panel. The computations for gamma kernels are done on the data divided by their mean distance from the boundary, so that they do not depend on the scale of the data. An observation exactly at a boundary gets no weight from these kernels anywhere inside the support, so such observations are rejected. The smoothing parameter is chosen by least-squares cross-validation of $\tilde f$, $\int \tilde f^2 - 2 \sum_i w_i \tilde f_{-i}(u_i)$, over a grid around the parameter whose kernel has the variance of a normal-reference bandwidth.

Transformation (Wand, Marron and Ruppert 1991), Transformed(). For a map $t$ of the support onto the real line, the estimate $\hat g$ of the density of $t(x_i)$, transformed back:

\[\hat f(x) = \hat g\big(t(x)\big)\, |t'(x)|, \qquad \hat F(x) = \hat G\big(t(x)\big),\]

with $t(x) = \log(x - a)$ for $[a, \infty)$, $-\log(b - x)$ for $(-\infty, b]$, and the logit or probit of $(x - a)/(b - a)$ for $[a, b]$. The estimate is a distribution exactly, its quantiles and draws come from those of $\hat g$, and its effective bandwidth grows with the distance from the boundary. It suits densities that are steep or infinite at a boundary, which the other methods smooth away.

Example

using KernelSmoothing, Distributions

x = rand(Exponential(), 500)
f = fit(BoundedKDE, x; support = (0, Inf))                          # reflection
g = fit(BoundedKDE, x; support = (0, Inf), method = GammaKernels())
pdf(f, 0.0), pdf(g, 0.0), cdf(g, 1.0), quantile(g, 0.9)

y = rand(Beta(0.5, 0.5), 500)                                       # infinite at both ends
fit(BoundedKDE, y; support = (0, 1), method = Transformed())

Reference

KernelSmoothing.BoundedKDE — Type
BoundedKDE <: ContinuousUnivariateDistribution

A kernel density estimate on a support [a, b] with one or two finite boundaries, which is a distribution on the support: its density integrates to one there, and pdf, logpdf, cdf, ccdf, quantile, rand, mean, var, minimum, maximum and insupport work as for any distribution of Distributions.jl. Fitted with fit(BoundedKDE, x; support, method), see BoundaryMethod for the methods. bandwidth(f) returns the bandwidth, or the smoothing parameter of beta and gamma kernels.

Unlike KDE, which has no boundary correction, the estimate does not spill mass across the boundaries. For estimates of the density at a boundary with robust bias-corrected intervals, see LocalPolynomialDensity.

StatsAPI.fit — Method
fit(BoundedKDE, x; support, method = Reflection(), kernel = EpanechnikovKernel(),
    bandwidth = nothing, weights = nothing)
fit(BoundedKDE, d::SmoothingData; support, method, kernel, bandwidth)

The kernel density estimate of the sample x on the support = (a, b), a pair with a < b of which at least one is finite, such as (0, Inf), (-Inf, 0) or (0, 1), with the boundary correction method: Reflection, BetaKernels, GammaKernels or Transformed. The observations must lie in the support. kernel is the kernel of reflection and transformation estimates; beta and gamma kernels have their own.

bandwidth is a positive number or a rule. By default it is SheatherJones applied to the observations and their first mirror images for reflection and to the transformed observations for transformation estimates. For beta and gamma kernels it is the smoothing parameter, chosen by default by least-squares cross-validation, ∫ f̃² - 2 ∑ᵢ wᵢ f̃₋ᵢ(uᵢ)/W, at the global minimum over the gridsize values of an LSCV rule (by default 30), spaced evenly on the log scale from 1/30 to 5 times the parameter whose kernel has the variance of a normal-reference bandwidth, with a warning when the minimum lies at an end.

KernelSmoothing.Reflection — Type
Reflection()

Reflection at the boundaries (Schuster 1985). With a lower boundary a, the estimate is

f̂(x) = ∑ᵢ wᵢ (k((x - xᵢ)/h) + k((x + xᵢ - 2a)/h)) / (h W),   x ≥ a,

the kernel estimate of the observations and their mirror images, restricted to the support, and symmetrically for an upper boundary. With two boundaries a < b, the images are reflected at both, repeatedly, so that f̂ is the density of xᵢ + h ε folded into [a, b], for an observation xᵢ drawn by weight and ε from the kernel. The estimate integrates to one and is computed exactly, with its distribution function. Its derivative vanishes at a boundary, so that it has a bias of order h there unless the density's derivative vanishes too. The kernel must be symmetric; a bandwidth rule is applied to the observations and their first images, which targets the bandwidth of the density on the support.

KernelSmoothing.BetaKernels — Type
BetaKernels(; modified = false)

The beta kernel estimator of Chen (1999) on [a, b]: with u = (x - a)/(b - a) and uᵢ = (xᵢ - a)/(b - a),

f̃(x) = ∑ᵢ wᵢ β(uᵢ; u/s + 1, (1 - u)/s + 1) / ((b - a) W),

where β(⋅; p, q) is the density of the Beta(p, q) distribution and s the smoothing parameter, given in bandwidth. The kernel changes its shape with the point, so that it never puts mass outside the support, and the bias is of order s everywhere. With modified = true, the second estimator of Chen (1999), whose interior bias does not involve f': β(⋅; u/s, (1 - u)/s) for u ∈ [2s, 1 - 2s], with the first parameter replaced by ρ(u) = 2s² + 2.5 - √(4s⁴ + 6s² + 2.25 - u² - u/s) for u < 2s and the second by ρ(1 - u) for u > 1 - 2s; it needs s < 1/4. Neither integrates to one; the estimate is f̃ divided by its integral, computed by quadrature, which also gives the distribution function.

An observation exactly at a boundary would get no weight anywhere inside the support, since the kernels vanish there, so such observations are rejected; shift the support slightly or use Reflection.

source
KernelSmoothing.GammaKernels — Type
GammaKernels(; modified = false)

The gamma kernel estimator of Chen (2000) on [a, ∞): with u = x - a and uᵢ = xᵢ - a,

f̃(x) = ∑ᵢ wᵢ γ(uᵢ; u/s + 1, s) / W,

where γ(⋅; κ, s) is the density of the gamma distribution with shape κ and scale s, the smoothing parameter given in bandwidth. With modified = true, the second estimator of Chen (2000), with shape u/s for u ≥ 2s and (u/(2s))² + 1 below. As for BetaKernels, the estimate is f̃ divided by its integral, and observations exactly at the boundary are rejected. A support (-∞, b] is handled by reflecting the data at b.

source
KernelSmoothing.Transformed — Type
Transformed(transform = :auto)

The transformation estimator (Wand, Marron and Ruppert 1991; Marron and Ruppert 1994): the kernel density estimate ĝ of t(xᵢ) for a map t of the support onto the real line, transformed back,

f̂(x) = ĝ(t(x)) |t'(x)|,   F̂(x) = Ĝ(t(x)),

with t(x) = log(x - a) for a support [a, ∞) (transform = :log), -log(b - x) for (-∞, b], and t(x) = log((x - a)/(b - x)) (:logit) or Φ⁻¹((x - a)/(b - a)) (:probit) for [a, b]; :auto takes :log or :logit. The estimate is a distribution exactly, its quantiles and draws come from those of ĝ, and the bandwidth, chosen on the transformed data and given on that scale, varies with x on the original scale. It suits densities that are infinite or steep at a boundary. The observations must lie strictly inside the support.
