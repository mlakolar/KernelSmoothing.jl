Density estimation

fit(KDE, x) returns a kernel density estimate that is a continuous univariate distribution of Distributions.jl. Its density and distribution function are computed exactly from the observations, without a grid; broadcasting over many points uses the engines of kernelsums. Bandwidths are numbers or BandwidthSelectors.

KernelSmoothing.KDE — Type
KDE <: ContinuousUnivariateDistribution

A kernel density estimate

f̂(x) = ∑ᵢ wᵢ k((x - xᵢ) / h) / (h ∑ᵢ wᵢ),

fitted with fit(KDE, x). A KDE is a continuous univariate distribution of Distributions.jl, so pdf, logpdf, cdf, quantile, rand, mean, var, minimum, maximum and insupport work as for any other distribution. The density and the distribution function are computed exactly from the observations, without a grid. Broadcasting pdf.(f, xs), logpdf.(f, xs), cdf.(f, xs) or stderror.(f, xs) over many points computes them with kernelsums, which is much faster than evaluating point by point.

derivative(f, r) estimates the r-th derivative of the density, stderror(f, x) gives the standard error of f̂(x) and bandwidth(f) the bandwidth.

KernelSmoothing.derivative — Function
derivative(f::KDE, r = 1)

The estimate of the r-th derivative of the density,

f̂⁽ʳ⁾(x) = ∑ᵢ wᵢ k⁽ʳ⁾((x - xᵢ) / h) / (h^(r + 1) ∑ᵢ wᵢ),

the r-th derivative of f̂, as a function: derivative(f, r)(x), and derivative(f, r).(xs) for many points at once. The kernel must have r derivatives that are functions, see smoothness: the Epanechnikov kernel allows r = 1, the biweight kernel r ≤ 2 and the Gaussian kernel any order. Derivatives of a density are estimated best with a larger bandwidth than the density itself.

StatsAPI.fit — Method
fit(KDE, x; kernel = EpanechnikovKernel(), bandwidth = NormalReference(), weights = nothing, engine = nothing)
fit(KDE, d::SmoothingData; kernel, bandwidth, engine)

The kernel density estimate of the sample x, with optional weights, or of a prepared sample d. The bandwidth is a positive number or a BandwidthSelector; with the default, NormalReference, it minimizes the asymptotic mean integrated squared error for normal data. It is a half-width for the compactly supported kernels and a standard deviation for the Gaussian kernel.

The kernel must be a probability density, nonnegative and integrating to one, so that the estimate is one too. Kernels that take negative values, such as higher-order kernels and derivatives of kernels, are rejected; for derivatives of the density, see derivative(f, r).

engine fixes the SumEngine that evaluates the estimate at many points at once; by default the faster exact engine is chosen for each evaluation.

Examples

julia> f = fit(KDE, [0.0, 1.0, 1.5, 3.0]; bandwidth = 1.0);

julia> pdf(f, 1.0), cdf(f, 1.0)
(0.328125, 0.4140625)

julia> quantile(f, 0.5)
1.25

StatsAPI.stderror — Method
stderror(f::KDE, x)

The standard error of the density estimate f̂(x),

√(∑ᵢ wᵢ² (gᵢ - f̂(x))²) / ∑ᵢ wᵢ,   gᵢ = k((x - xᵢ) / h) / h,

the plug-in estimate of its exact variance for independent observations; with equal weights, the sample variance of the gᵢ divided by n. It measures how f̂(x) varies around its expectation, not its bias, so f̂(x) ± 1.96 stderror(f, x) covers the density only for bandwidths small enough that the bias is negligible, which the bandwidths that minimize the mean squared error are not.

KernelSmoothing.BandwidthSelector — Type
BandwidthSelector

Supertype of the rules that choose a bandwidth from the data, such as NormalReference and SilvermanRule. selectbandwidth applies a rule and records what it computed on the way, and bandwidth returns only the bandwidth. Estimators accept a rule wherever they accept a bandwidth.

A new rule R <: BandwidthSelector implements selectbandwidth(rule::R, d::SmoothingData, k::AbstractKernel).

source
KernelSmoothing.NormalReference — Type
NormalReference(; robust = true)

The bandwidth that minimizes the asymptotic mean integrated squared error when the density is normal,

h = σ̂ (8 √π R(k) / (3 μ₂(k)² n))^(1/5),

where R(k) = roughness(k), μ₂(k) = moment(k, 2) and n is the effective_nobs. The scale σ̂ is the standard deviation or, with robust = true, the smaller of the standard deviation and the interquartile range divided by 1.349, which guards against heavy tails and outliers. For the Gaussian kernel the rule gives 1.06 σ̂ n^(-1/5) and for the Epanechnikov kernel 2.34 σ̂ n^(-1/5).

The rule oversmooths densities that are far from normal, multimodal ones in particular. It is a quick default and a pilot for better selectors. It needs a second-order kernel, with moment(k, 2) > 0.

source
KernelSmoothing.SilvermanRule — Type
SilvermanRule()

Silverman's rule of thumb, 0.9 min(σ̂, IQR / 1.34) n^(-1/5) for the Gaussian kernel, where σ̂ is the standard deviation, IQR the interquartile range and n the effective_nobs. It is the default bandwidth of R's density (bw.nrd0) and of KernelDensity.jl. Other kernels get the bandwidth that R's density uses for them, the Gaussian one divided by the standard deviation of the kernel, sqrt(moment(k, 2)). Compared with NormalReference, the factor 0.9 instead of 1.06 suits densities with several modes better.
