Density estimation

Kernel density estimates

For observations $x_1, \dots, x_n$ with weights $w_i$, a kernel $K$ and a bandwidth $h$, the kernel density estimate is

\[\hat f(x) = \frac{1}{hW} \sum_{i=1}^n w_i K\Big(\frac{x - x_i}{h}\Big), \qquad W = \sum_{i=1}^n w_i .\]

fit(KDE, x) returns it as a continuous univariate distribution of Distributions.jl, so that pdf, logpdf, cdf, ccdf, quantile, cquantile, rand, mean, var, std, median, minimum, maximum and insupport work as for any distribution. KernelSmoothing exports the functions among these that it extends or that a summary of a density needs, mean, var, std, median and cquantile included, so that they work without using Distributions; they are the functions of Distributions.jl and Statistics, so that loading those packages as well causes no conflict. The density is computed exactly from the observations, without a grid. A point costs a binary search in the sorted data and a sum over the observations within $h$ of it, and broadcasting pdf.(f, xs) over many points uses the engines of kernel_sums, also inside fused broadcasts such as pdf.(f, xs .+ 1). Points that are not plain numbers, such as the dual numbers of ForwardDiff.jl, go through the scalar methods, so that the estimate can be differentiated automatically. Bandwidths are numbers or BandwidthSelectors, by default SheatherJones; see Bandwidth selection.

The distribution function is $\hat F(x) = W^{-1} \sum_i w_i \mathcal{K}((x - x_i)/h)$, where $\mathcal{K}$ is the distribution function of the kernel, and quantile(f, p) is $\inf\{x : \hat F(x) \geq p\}$, found by safeguarded Newton steps. ccdf(f, x) sums the upper tail $1 - \hat F(x)$ directly, and cquantile(f, p) inverts it, so that both keep their relative accuracy far above the data, where $1 - \hat F(x)$ computed from $\hat F(x)$ would round to zero. A draw from the estimate is an observation chosen with probability proportional to its weight plus $h$ times a draw from the kernel, the smoothed bootstrap. The estimate of the derivative of order $r$,

\[\hat f^{(r)}(x) = \frac{1}{h^{r+1} W} \sum_{i=1}^n w_i K^{(r)}\Big(\frac{x - x_i}{h}\Big),\]

is available as derivative(f, r) for $r$ up to the smoothness of the kernel.

stderror(f, x) is the plug-in estimate of the exact standard deviation of $\hat f(x)$ for independent observations,

\[\widehat{\mathrm{se}}(x) = \frac{1}{W} \Big( \sum_{i=1}^n w_i^2 \big(g_i - \hat f(x)\big)^2 \Big)^{1/2}, \qquad g_i = \frac{1}{h} K\Big(\frac{x - x_i}{h}\Big),\]

which with equal weights is the sample variance of the $g_i$ divided by $n$. It ignores the bias, so that $\hat f(x) \pm 1.96\, \widehat{\mathrm{se}}(x)$ covers $f(x)$ only for bandwidths well below the ones that minimize the mean squared error. The robust bias-corrected intervals below account for the bias, and confint(f, points) gives them at the bandwidth of the estimate.

A kernel density estimate with a kernel that takes negative values is not a density: it is negative wherever the negative lobes dominate, which breaks logpdf, the monotonicity of the distribution function, quantile and rand. Therefore, fit(KDE, …) accepts only nonnegative kernels that integrate to one. Higher-order and derivative kernels are available through derivative(f, r) and the pointwise estimators.

KernelSmoothing.KDE — Type
KDE <: ContinuousUnivariateDistribution

A kernel density estimate

f̂(x) = ∑ᵢ wᵢ k((x - xᵢ) / h) / (h ∑ᵢ wᵢ),

fitted with fit(KDE, x). A KDE is a continuous univariate distribution of Distributions.jl, so pdf, logpdf, cdf, ccdf, quantile, cquantile, rand, mean, var, minimum, maximum and insupport work as for any other distribution. The density and the distribution function are computed exactly from the observations, without a grid; ccdf and cquantile sum the upper tail directly, so that they keep their relative accuracy far above the data for the named and piecewise polynomial kernels, the Gaussian kernel and kernels that define ccdf. Broadcasting pdf.(f, xs), logpdf.(f, xs), cdf.(f, xs), ccdf.(f, xs) or stderror.(f, xs) over many points computes them for all points at once, with kernel_sums or one pass over the windows, which is much faster than evaluating point by point, also inside fused broadcasts, and gives the same values up to rounding; points that are not plain numbers, such as dual numbers, use the scalar methods, so that automatic differentiation works.

derivative(f, r) estimates the r-th derivative of the density, stderror(f, x) gives the standard error of f̂(x) and bandwidth(f) the bandwidth.

KernelSmoothing.derivative — Function
derivative(f::KDE, r = 1)

The estimate of the r-th derivative of the density,

f̂⁽ʳ⁾(x) = ∑ᵢ wᵢ k⁽ʳ⁾((x - xᵢ) / h) / (h^(r + 1) ∑ᵢ wᵢ),

the r-th derivative of f̂, as a function: derivative(f, r)(x), and derivative(f, r).(xs) for many points at once. The kernel must have r derivatives that are functions, see smoothness: the Epanechnikov kernel allows r = 1, the biweight kernel r ≤ 2 and the Gaussian kernel any order. Derivatives of a density are estimated best with a larger bandwidth than the density itself.

StatsAPI.fit — Method
fit(KDE, x; kernel = EpanechnikovKernel(), bandwidth = SheatherJones(), weights = nothing, engine = nothing)
fit(KDE, d::SmoothingData; kernel, bandwidth, engine)

The kernel density estimate of the sample x, with optional weights, or of a prepared sample d. The bandwidth is a positive number or a BandwidthSelector; the default (also for bandwidth = nothing), SheatherJones, is the plug-in bandwidth that studies found the most reliable for densities without boundaries. It is a half-width for the compactly supported kernels and a standard deviation for the Gaussian kernel.

The kernel must be a probability density, nonnegative and integrating to one, so that the estimate is one too. Kernels that take negative values, such as higher-order kernels and derivatives of kernels, are rejected; for derivatives of the density, see derivative(f, r).

engine fixes the SumEngine that evaluates the estimate at many points at once; by default the faster exact engine is chosen for each evaluation.

Examples

julia> f = fit(KDE, [0.0, 1.0, 1.5, 3.0]; bandwidth = 1.0);

julia> pdf(f, 1.0), cdf(f, 1.0)
(0.328125, 0.4140625)

julia> quantile(f, 0.5)
1.25

StatsAPI.stderror — Method
stderror(f::KDE, x)

The standard error of the density estimate f̂(x),

√(∑ᵢ wᵢ² (gᵢ - f̂(x))²) / ∑ᵢ wᵢ,   gᵢ = k((x - xᵢ) / h) / h,

the plug-in estimate of its exact variance for independent observations; with equal weights, the variance of the gᵢ with divisor n, divided by n. It measures how f̂(x) varies around its expectation, not its bias, so f̂(x) ± 1.96 stderror(f, x) covers the density only for bandwidths small enough that the bias is negligible, which the bandwidths that minimize the mean squared error are not.

Inference

PointwiseDensity estimates the density at a set of points with conventional and robust bias-corrected standard errors and intervals (Calonico, Cattaneo and Farrell 2018). For a second-order kernel, the leading bias of $\hat f(x)$ is $h^2 \mu_2(K) f''(x)/2$, where $\mu_j(K) = \int u^j K(u)\,du$. We estimate $f''$ with a bandwidth $b$ and the kernel

\[D(u) = K(u) (c_0 + c_2 u^2), \qquad c_2 = \frac{2}{\mu_4(K) - \mu_2(K)^2}, \qquad c_0 = -\mu_2(K) c_2 ,\]

which is the equivalent kernel of the second derivative in a local quadratic fit with kernel $K$. This gives the bias-corrected estimate

\[\hat f^{\mathrm{bc}}(x) = \frac{1}{hW} \sum_{i=1}^n w_i M\Big(\frac{x - x_i}{h}\Big), \qquad M(u) = K(u) - \rho^3 \frac{\mu_2(K)}{2} D(\rho u), \qquad \rho = \frac{h}{b} .\]

The kernel $D$ satisfies $\int u^j D(u)\,du = 2 \delta_{j2}$ for $j \leq 3$, so that $M$ is a fourth-order kernel for $\rho = 1$. The robust standard error is the exact standard deviation of this linear estimator, estimated as for KDE, so that it includes the variability of the bias estimate. By default $b = h$. nprobust 1.0.0 multiplies $D$ by $\int u^4 D(u)\,du / 24$ instead of $\mu_2(K)/2$, which is $1/18$ instead of $1/10$ for the Epanechnikov kernel and removes only 5/9 of the leading bias. compat = true reproduces it. Where no observation has positive weight in a window, the standard error is NaN rather than nprobust's zero, which gave intervals of width zero far from the data, and the lower ends of the intervals are clamped at zero, since the density is nonnegative.

The default bandwidths are plug-in estimates of the bandwidths that minimize the asymptotic mean squared error at each point, or its integral over the points,

\[h(x) = \left( \frac{R(K)\, \hat f(x)}{4n \big(\hat B(x)^2 + \hat R(x)\big)} \right)^{1/5}, \qquad \hat B(x) = \frac{\mu_2(K)}{2} \hat f''(x), \qquad \hat R(x) = 3 \Big(\frac{\mu_2(K)}{2}\Big)^2 \hat V\big[\hat f''(x)\big],\]

where $R(K) = \int K(u)^2\,du$. The regularization $\hat R$, the counterpart of the one in nprobust's regression bandwidths, keeps the bandwidth finite where $f''$ vanishes. We estimate $f$ at the Sheather–Jones bandwidth $h_{SJ}$ and $f''$ at $b = h_{SJ}\, b_{NR}/h_{NR}$. Here $b_{NR}$ and $h_{NR}$ are the normal-reference bandwidths for $f''$, with the kernel $D$ and the rate $n^{-1/9}$, and for $f$. Since $h_{SJ}$ adapts to the roughness of the density, so does the pilot. With compat = true, the pilots are nprobust's normal-reference ones, which can be far too wide for multimodal densities.

x = randn(1000)
m = fit(PointwiseDensity, x; points = [-1, 0, 1])   # plug-in bandwidths at each point
confint(m)                                          # robust bias-corrected 95% intervals
confint(fit(KDE, x), [-1, 0, 1])                    # at the Sheather–Jones bandwidth
KernelSmoothing.PointwiseDensity — Type
PointwiseDensity <: StatisticalModel

Kernel density estimates at a set of points with conventional and robust bias-corrected inference, fitted with fit(PointwiseDensity, x).

At a point x₀, the estimate is f̂(x₀) = ∑ᵢ wᵢ k((x₀ - xᵢ)/h) / (h W) with W = ∑ᵢ wᵢ. The bias-corrected estimate subtracts its leading bias h² μ₂(k)/2 f''(x₀), with f'' estimated at the pilot bandwidth b:

f̂ᵇᶜ(x₀) = ∑ᵢ wᵢ M((x₀ - xᵢ)/h) / (h W),   M(u) = k(u) - ρ³ μ₂(k)/2 D(ρu),   ρ = h/b,

where D(u) = k(u) (c₀ + c₂ u²) is the kernel of the second derivative in a local quadratic fit with kernel k. With ρ = 1, M is a fourth-order kernel. The standard errors estimate the exact variances of the two linear estimators, so that the robust one includes the variability of the bias estimate. The estimates are inconsistent at boundary points, so evaluate in the interior.

coef(m; method), stderror(m; method), confint(m; level, method) and coeftable(m) work as for LocalPolynomialRegression; the column nobs of coeftable counts the observations with positive weight in the larger of the windows of h and b, as for regression, or with compat = true all those within max(h, b), as nprobust.

StatsAPI.confint — Method
confint(f::KDE, x; level = 0.95, method = RobustBiasCorrected())

Pointwise confidence intervals for the density at the points x, with the bandwidth of f and the same pilot bandwidth, as rows [lower upper] of a matrix. The robust bias-corrected intervals (Calonico, Cattaneo and Farrell 2018) remain valid at bandwidths that minimize the mean squared error; method = Conventional() gives f̂(x) ± z stderror(f, x), which covers only when the bias is negligible. The lower ends are clamped at zero, and where no observation has positive weight the interval is NaN. See PointwiseDensity for pointwise bandwidths.

StatsAPI.fit — Method
fit(PointwiseDensity, x; points = nothing, kernel = EpanechnikovKernel(), bandwidth = nothing,
    pilot = nothing, rho = 1, minobs = 21, weights = nothing, compat = false)
fit(PointwiseDensity, d::SmoothingData; kwargs...)

Estimates the density of the sample x at points, by default the quantiles of order 0.1, …, 0.9 at 30 equally spaced levels, with conventional and robust bias-corrected standard errors (Calonico, Cattaneo and Farrell 2018), as nprobust's kdrobust does.

  • bandwidth is a number, a vector with one bandwidth per point, or a BandwidthSelector. By default it is the plug-in bandwidth, MSEOptimal at one point and IMSEOptimal at several, with pilots that adapt to the density through the Sheather–Jones bandwidth.
  • pilot is the bandwidth b of the estimate of f''; by default b = h / rho. With pilot = :selected, the pilot bandwidth of the plug-in rule is used.
  • minobs raises h and b at each point to the distance of the minobs-th nearest observation, where needed; minobs = 0 turns this off.
  • compat = true reproduces nprobust 1.0.0: its bias correction uses the constant ∫ u⁴ D(u) du / 24 instead of μ₂(k)/2, which removes only 5/9 of the leading bias for the Epanechnikov kernel, and its plug-in bandwidths use normal-reference pilots and, when integrated, square the average bias. nprobust's kdrobust accepts the Epanechnikov and uniform kernels only, so that compat = true with another kernel is an error.

Where no observation has positive weight in a window, the standard error of that estimate is NaN: nprobust gives zero, and with it an interval of width zero around an estimate of zero, as at points far from the data. The lower ends of the intervals and bands are clamped at zero, since the density is nonnegative, which can only raise their coverage. With compat = true, both are as in nprobust.

The kernel must be a symmetric second-order density kernel.

At boundaries

A kernel density estimate is inconsistent at a boundary of the support, since the kernel puts part of its mass outside. LocalPolynomialDensity is the estimator of Cattaneo, Jansson and Ma (2020), as R's lpdensity computes it, which needs no knowledge of where the boundary lies. At a point $x_0$, it regresses the empirical distribution function $\hat F$ on a polynomial of order $p$ with kernel weights, over the distinct values $u_g$ with total weights $P_g$,

\[\hat\beta = \arg\min_\beta \sum_g P_g K_h(u_g - x_0) \Big( \hat F(u_g) - \sum_{j=0}^p \beta_j \Big(\frac{u_g - x_0}{h}\Big)^j \Big)^2 .\]

The coefficient $v!\, \hat\beta_v / h^v$ estimates the derivative of order $v$ of $F$, so that $v = 1$ gives the density and derivative = v - 1 its derivatives. Since the polynomial adapts to the data on the side of $x_0$ where they lie, the estimate keeps its rate at a boundary. The bias-corrected estimate is the fit of order $p + 1$ with the same bandwidth. Since $\hat F$ is estimated from the same data, the standard errors come from the influence function of the estimate, a U-statistic, which reverse cumulative sums over the distinct values compute in one pass.

The default bandwidth, MSEOptimal, is the plug-in estimate of lpdensity. It minimizes over the bandwidth $a$, on the data standardized by their median and a robust scale, the estimated mean squared error

\[a^{2p + 2 - 2v} \big( |B_1| + a\, |B_2| \big)^2 + \frac{V}{a^{2v - 1}} ,\]

where $B_1$ and $B_2$ are the first two terms of the bias and $V$ is the variance constant. The bias terms come from local estimates of $F^{(p+1)}$ and $F^{(p+2)}$ at pilot bandwidths, rules of thumb integrated over the quantiles of the data and floored at each point. lpdensity differs in three ways, which compat = true restores. First, it standardizes by the mean and the standard deviation, which one outlier inflates. Second, it integrates the pilots over the evaluation points, so that one far point changes the bandwidths at all the others. Third, it uses $(B_1 + a B_2)^2$, which vanishes at $a = -B_1/B_2$ when the estimates have opposite signs, so that it can choose bandwidths of the order of the range of the data. NormalReference() gives the rule of thumb for normal data, with the next term of the bias, which keeps the bandwidth finite at the inflection points of the normal density. The estimates at points outside the data whose nearest observation lies more than half a bandwidth away, where the fits extrapolate, are NaN, and the lower ends of the intervals for the density are clamped at zero.

using Random
x = randexp(5000)
m = fit(LocalPolynomialDensity, x; points = [0.0, 0.5, 1.0])   # consistent at the boundary 0
confint(m)
KernelSmoothing.LocalPolynomialDensity — Type
LocalPolynomialDensity <: StatisticalModel

Local polynomial density estimates (Cattaneo, Jansson and Ma 2020) at a set of points, with conventional and robust bias-corrected inference, fitted with fit(LocalPolynomialDensity, x), as R's lpdensity computes them.

At a point x₀, the empirical distribution function F̂, with tied observations at the value at the end of their run, is regressed on a polynomial of order p in (x - x₀)/h with kernel weights over the distinct values. The coefficient of order ν + 1 estimates the derivative f⁽ᵛ⁾(x₀) of the density. Since the fit adapts to the data near x₀, the estimate needs no knowledge of a boundary and keeps its rate there, where kernel density estimates are inconsistent. The bias-corrected estimate is the fit of order p + 1 with the same bandwidth. The standard errors come from the influence function of F̂ on the fit, so that they account for its estimation.

coef(m; method), stderror(m; method), confint(m; level, method), coeftable(m), vcov and conf_band work as for PointwiseDensity. The columns nobs and nunique of coeftable count the observations and the distinct values with positive weight in the window of each point, or with compat = true all those within h, as lpdensity's nh and nhu.

StatsAPI.fit — Method
fit(LocalPolynomialDensity, x; points = nothing, degree = 2, derivative = 0,
    kernel = TriangularKernel(), bandwidth = MSEOptimal(), minobs = degree + 21, weights = nothing,
    compat = false)
fit(LocalPolynomialDensity, d::SmoothingData; kwargs...)

Estimates the density of x, or its derivative of order derivative, at points (by default the quantiles of order 0.05, 0.10, …, 0.95, weighted for weighted data) with local polynomial fits of order degree to the empirical distribution function, and the robust bias-corrected estimates of order degree + 1. The estimates are NaN at points outside the range of the data whose nearest observation lies more than half a bandwidth away, where the fits extrapolate; a natural boundary just beyond the data, as 0 for positive data, keeps its estimate.

  • bandwidth is a number, a vector with one bandwidth per point, or a rule of lpdensity: MSEOptimal(), its plug-in estimate of the bandwidth that minimizes the asymptotic mean squared error at each point (bwselect = "mse-dpi", the default, which nothing also selects); IMSEOptimal(), one plug-in bandwidth that minimizes the sum of these errors over the points ("imse-dpi"), or with grid = m over m equally spaced points between the smallest and the largest observation; or NormalReference(), the rule of thumb for normal data ("mse-rot"). The plug-in rules estimate the derivatives of orders degree + 1 and degree + 2 of the distribution function with local fits at rule-of-thumb pilot bandwidths, and the constants of the bias and the variance from the data.
  • minobs raises selected bandwidths at each point, where needed, so that the window holds minobs observations and minobs distinct values, as lpdensity's nLocalMin and nUniqueMin; minobs = 0 turns this off. The rules apply the floor before they cap the bandwidth at the distance to the farthest distinct value, as lpdensity does, and IMSEOptimal applies the largest floor over the evaluation points to its single bandwidth. Given bandwidths are not raised, as in lpdensity, and the fit warns where they fall below the floor.

The rules depart from lpdensity's in three ways. First, they standardize the data by the median and the robust scale of NormalReference instead of the mean and the standard deviation, so that one outlier does not inflate every bandwidth; NormalReference(robust = false) keeps the standard deviation. Second, the rules of thumb for the pilot bandwidths are integrated over the quantiles of the data and floored at each point, while lpdensity integrates them over the evaluation points, so that one far point changes the bandwidths at all the others. Third, the plug-in rules bound the squared bias by (|B₁| + a |B₂|)², since lpdensity's (B₁ + a B₂)² vanishes at a = -B₁/B₂ when the estimated terms have opposite signs, and the rules of thumb add the next term of the bias, which keeps it from vanishing at the inflection points of the normal density, where lpdensity's bandwidth jumps to the range of the data. The lower ends of the intervals for the density itself are clamped at zero.

With compat = true, the fit agrees with lpdensity 3.0.1 with massPoints = TRUE. The default points are unweighted quantiles of the input, before observations with zero weight are dropped. Points outside the data are extrapolated, the rules are lpdensity's, plug-in fits whose matrix R's solve rejects as ill-conditioned fall back to the floors, and intervals are not clamped. For a prepared SmoothingData, the default points use its retained observations, since observations it already dropped are unavailable. The kernel must be compactly supported and nonnegative; lpdensity uses the triangular, Epanechnikov and uniform kernels.
